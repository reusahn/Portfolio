01 / CAPTURE ARCHITECTURE

Separate Body & Facial Performance Capture

The dancers’ physical performance was too important to reduce to one generic capture stream. Body and facial performance were captured separately with tools suited to each signal, then reconciled later in Unreal.

CAPTURE / PERFORMANCE TESTVIMEO · AUTOPLAY · TIMELINE + VOLUME
Task 01A / Body Capture

Capturing large dancer-driven movement with MOVIN TRACIN

All three performers were dancers, and the movement included strong full-body actions while fabric was pressed and rubbed across the body. MOVIN TRACIN was used as the body-performance source for the three performers.

Task 01B / Facial Capture

Preserving facial performance as an independent stream

Facial acting was captured separately using a Rokoko head-mounted setup and iPhone facial capture, with Live Link Face / MetaHuman Animator used in the facial-performance pipeline.

Rokoko head rig + iPhone.

Live Link Face / MetaHuman Animator in the Unreal facial workflow.

Body and facial capture could be evaluated and aligned independently.

Retain expressive facial motion alongside the dancers’ large body movement.

CHAPTER OUTCOME

The capture architecture preserved body and facial performance as separate production assets, giving the Unreal assembly stage control over their timing rather than locking both to one capture pass.

02 / PERFORMANCE ASSEMBLY

Manual Body / Face Synchronization in Unreal

The body and facial streams did not arrive as one automatically synchronized performance. I aligned the two tracks manually for each dancer so the final character read as one continuous performance.

INTEGRATED CHARACTER PERFORMANCEVIMEO · BODY + FACE RESULT
Task 02A / Per-Performer Alignment

Synchronizing three independent performances one performer at a time

I manually adjusted body/face timing for each dancer in Unreal. The alignment was judged in the context of the performer’s movement rather than assuming one fixed offset would work across all three characters.

Body and face aligned as one individual performance.

Timing adjusted independently rather than copied from another dancer.

Separate alignment pass for the third performance.

Final synchronized character performance evaluated in Unreal.

Task 02B / Integrated Character Motion

Synchronization was not the end deliverable. The assembled performance had to be converted into deforming character geometry so the next department could use the moving body and face as a simulation surface.

Task 03B / Department Boundary

Delivering the correct asset without overstating downstream ownership

My responsibility ended at the synchronized, simulation-ready animated geometry. The downstream Houdini cloth simulation was performed by the next department / collaborator, not by me.

Downstream context — Cloth-contact simulation was completed by the downstream Houdini team using the animated geometry delivered from this pipeline.
Production value

The handoff translated performance-capture data into the representation the simulation department actually needed: moving body and face geometry that could drive cloth contact and imprint behavior.

CHAPTER OUTCOME

The final deliverable demonstrates cross-DCC production thinking: capture quality mattered, but the pipeline was only successful once the next department received geometry that could be used directly for simulation.

TAKEAWAY

The technical contribution was the bridge between capture and simulation: three dancers, separate body and facial streams, per-performer synchronization in Unreal, and full-character Alembic geometry prepared for downstream Houdini work.
