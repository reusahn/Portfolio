OVERVIEW

The problem changed when the camera stopped being the director.

The original material was performance-driven and cinematic. In VR, the viewer can look anywhere, so timing, camera motion, lighting, and visual effects had to support self-directed attention instead of assuming a fixed frame.

01 / USER-CENTERED ITERATION

Re-editing movement for headset comfort

A camera move that feels acceptable on a monitor can become uncomfortable in a headset.

The original camera motion was too fast for comfortable VR viewing.

Treat camera speed as an interaction/comfort variable rather than a purely cinematic choice.

Tested the VR experience informally with roughly ten viewers and reduced camera speed based on observed comfort.

The viewer remained the perceptual center; the scene and performance had to do more of the directional work.

Result

The VR edit prioritized a stable, readable viewing experience while preserving the performance.

02 / SPATIAL UX

Guiding attention without forcing the viewer's camera

Because the viewer could look away at any moment, important action needed a spatial cue rather than a forced cut.

Traditional framing could no longer guarantee where the viewer looked.

Use lighting and scene contrast as directional cues.

Built the VR scene around dusk/HDRI lighting and used brighter areas to reinforce the character's location and motion.

Direction had to remain suggestive, not restrictive, because gaze still belonged to the viewer.

Result

Lighting became part of the interaction design rather than only a look-development layer.

03 / REAL-TIME EFFECT

Hand-driven ribbon trails as readable motion feedback

The hands needed a real-time visual accent that could remain legible from a freely moving viewpoint.

Fast hand movement could be easy to lose in a 360° scene.

Attach a lightweight real-time trail directly to hand motion.

Built Niagara ribbon emitters for both hands and attached them to the character so the effect followed captured motion.

The trail reinforced direction and rhythm without changing the underlying performance.

Result

The same captured animation became more spatially readable inside the headset.
