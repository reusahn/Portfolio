Escape Metro

A surreal Unity VR escape game where players solve puzzles and engage in real-time conversation with an in-game AI to navigate an uncanny subway system.

Role Director / Technical ArtistStack Unity · VR · ChatGPT API · Web3
Escape Metro16:9 · autoplay · timeline + volume
OVERVIEW

Conversation was not a menu layer; it was part of the way through the space.

The project connected a conventional VR escape-game loop with real-time AI dialogue. Players had to navigate, solve puzzles, and converse with an AI character as part of understanding the environment and progressing through the experience.

01 / XR GAMEPLAY

Connecting spatial puzzle solving and open-ended dialogue

The experience had to keep the player physically situated in a VR world while also making conversation meaningful to progression.

A conversational AI can easily feel detached from the actual game space.

Place AI conversation inside the escape loop rather than presenting it as a separate chatbot experience.

Built the experience in Unity and integrated a ChatGPT-powered in-game conversation layer alongside spatial exploration and puzzle solving.

The portfolio documents the product behavior and integration; it does not claim an undocumented custom LLM architecture.

Result

The AI became part of how players interpreted the subway's logic rather than an unrelated novelty.

02 / SHIPPING

Moving from prototype behavior to a public VR experience

A pop-up installation needs a repeatable experience that can be run for visitors, not only a developer-side prototype.

Presented at the Seongsu KAIA pop-up store.

Visitors needed a complete loop: enter the world, understand the interaction, converse, solve, and exit.

Treat runtime interaction and content as one delivered application.

The surviving project video shows the deployed VR experience and interaction context.

Result

The project was delivered as a public-facing VR game rather than remaining a lab demo.

TAKEAWAY

Escape Metro is representative of how I approach XR software: interaction design, engine implementation, content systems, and deployment are treated as one product problem.
