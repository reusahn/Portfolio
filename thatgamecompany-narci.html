04 / EXPERIMENTAL INTERACTION · VR

NARCI / Be NARCI.

A pair of Unity VR experiences using hand tracking, restricted space, reflection and role reversal. The player’s body and position are part of the interaction rather than simply controlling a viewpoint.

01 / PROBLEM & OWNERSHIP

A design problem translated into a runtime system.

The distinction between creative objective and technical contribution is central to this case.

Design / production challenge

In a confined VR space, the mechanics must communicate through body movement, gesture and environmental response. Ordinary UI prompts would break the intended sense of presence and controlled discomfort.

My technical contribution

I used Unity XR Interaction Toolkit and headset hand tracking for controller-free input, mapped gestures to runtime states, and authored reflective/material behaviors and scene pacing that respond to how the viewer occupies the space.

02 / IMPLEMENTATION

How the feature works.

Concrete subsystems and boundaries, not only a list of software tools.

Embodied player input

Touch, pinch and grab interactions use tracked hands rather than only controller buttons.

Environmental feedback

Custom material changes and reflection help communicate state in the immersive scene.

Narrative game design

Repetition, proximity and player agency establish the emotional rules of each piece.

In-headset iteration

Scale, lighting, reachability and pacing are tested at the actual user's viewpoint.

Hand tracking→XR interaction events→C# state changes→Materials / staging→Embodied feedback
03 / TECHNICAL DESIGN

Decisions that make iteration possible.

Each technical decision supports clarity, performance, or cross-disciplinary change.

Why hand tracking?

Direct gesture makes the character's relationship to the world tactile and part of the rules.

Why constrained space?

Limited movement intensifies attention and lets subtle scene changes communicate progression.

Why evaluate in VR?

Distance and timing that look correct in the editor can feel different when experienced at human scale.
