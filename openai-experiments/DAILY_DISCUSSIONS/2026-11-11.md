## A/B/C fictional discussion — one round
**A — Hiring reviewer:** “The previous coverage number (4/6) was useful, but a visitor looking at two columns still has to compare fact numbers manually. Make the change explicit: what became visible and what was removed? That gives a recruiter a faster proof of the artistic mechanic.”

**B — Hypothetical Creative, Experiments lead (responding to A):** “Agreed on legibility, but do not call it model inference. The provenance mapping is authored. We can compute a deterministic set difference between the two cuts and make it testable. This is not yet evidence that a generative model improves the experience.”

**C — Senior AI arts reviewer (challenging A and B):** “A before/after list can overstate objective truth. The six event facts are fixed by the director; the shot-to-fact labels are authored claims, not computer vision. Keep the ambiguity of motive. An edit may change emphasis without changing which facts are visible.”

**A → B:** “If shot order changes but the same facts are shown, the UI should say so, rather than imply new evidence.”

**B → C:** “That distinction becomes a regression test: reorder-only must produce empty newly-shown and newly-hidden sets; contextual inserts cannot count as facts.”

**C → A/B:** “Proceed, but continue labeling the system as an authored storyboard and avoid a numerical score suggesting verified artistic effectiveness.”

## Decision
Add a pure, deterministic **evidence delta** function and a plain-language comparison in the existing edit audit. Do not alter the canonical event ledger or imply live AI analysis. No numerical reviewer scores assigned: no browser test or user study validates audience comprehension.
