# Five Original Creative AI Experiment Concepts

**Captured November 3, 2026.** All five are **proposals**; inspiration image references in prior conversations are not the finished projects. The names and descriptions are working titles.

## 01 — The Things We Didn't Say
**Media:** AI · digital human · memory · interactive film. Audience types something they never said to someone. An AI proposes three different ways to convey it through an explicitly fictional performance, making visible the differences between intention, delivery and received meaning.
**Question:** What changes when AI expresses an emotion on someone's behalf?
**Strength:** Film direction, real-time digital humans, emotion and relationships.
**Original critique:** Merely rewriting text in three styles resembles an ordinary prompt demo. The audience needs editing agency and audiovisual consequence.
**Improved angle:** Visitor edits wording, silence and cadence; a consented synthetic performer changes expression, pauses and framing. Show difference between visitor-authored and machine-authored choices. Avoid identifying or impersonating real people.
**Status:** Specified in [THINGS_UNSAID_SPEC.md](THINGS_UNSAID_SPEC.md); not implemented.

## 02 — Museum of Almost Useful Things
**Media:** AI · humor · interactable objects. Everyday objects receive bizarre but plausible alternative functions: a cup that stores awkward silences; an umbrella that catches excuses.
**Question:** Does AI's absurd reinterpretation make ordinary life more imaginative?
**Strength:** Playfulness, clear immediate interaction, rapid prototyping.
**Original critique:** An amusing object description alone is interchangeable with chatbot output and not a substantial experience.
**Improved angle:** User manipulates virtual objects; their behaviors express AI's invented function, with unexpected consequences. Include physical feasibility / fictional-function labels.
**Status:** Ideation only; backlog.

## 03 — A Place That Remembers Differently
**Media:** AI · spatial storytelling · memory. Visitors describe a remembered place; its architecture, light and sound evolve as new details are added while traces of former descriptions remain.
**Question:** When memories are retold, do they still describe the same place?
**Strength:** Connects to INSUN, spatial capture, XR, lived experience.
**Original critique:** Generating a changing room is not sufficiently distinctive without an interaction rule or record of contradiction.
**Improved angle:** Layer past and present versions and expose contradictions. Make it possible to choose which version persists. Use synthetic memories unless participants explicitly consent.
**Status:** Ideation only; backlog.

## 04 — The AI That Misunderstands Me
**Media:** AI · gestures · humorous physical interaction. The audience offers gestures, an AI offers competing contextual readings, and visitor and agent revise their mutual understanding.
**Question:** What does it mean for two beings to understand each other?
**Strength:** Extends AI ZOO sensing, embodied interaction and miscommunication.
**Original critique:** A purposefully inaccurate AI is predictable, and facial expression alone is not a reliable emotion measurement.
**Improved angle:** Let the same gesture produce different contextual hypotheses; never claim to diagnose inner emotion from a face. Visitor clarifies, challenges and negotiates the reading.
**Status:** Ideation only; backlog.

## 05 — One Scene, Many Truths (selected first)
**Media:** AI · film editing · perspective · interactive experience. Same observable café event, three incompatible yet plausible witnesses: friend, café worker, and person who leaves the letter.
**Question:** How can truthful fragments produce different emotional narratives?
**Original critique:** Three fully regenerated films would risk changing factual events and become uncontrolled.
**Improved angle:** Preserve a signed immutable timeline of observable events. AI may select shots, pacing, subtitles, sound emphasis and motivated inferences **without rewriting event facts**. Let visitors compare perspectives and inspect what was known versus assumed.
**Status:** Priority 1; [full specification](ONE_SCENE_SPEC.md).

## Sequencing
- Priority 1: One Scene, Many Truths — concise, filmmaker-led interactive prototype with formal event invariants.
- Priority 2: The Things We Didn't Say — an emotional performance project that tests human vs model-authored expression.
- Priority 3: Museum of Almost Useful Things — small joyful demo for humor and broad public appeal.
- Priority 4: A Place That Remembers Differently — research-grade spatial memory iteration, higher cost.
- Priority 5: The AI That Misunderstands Me — return when embodied sensing / context constraints can be made robust.
