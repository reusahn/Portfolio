An apartment changes when one person is absent.

Urban Diary is an eighteen-minute short about a girl newly alone at home when her father returns to work. Based on the protagonist's poem, the film attends to domestic routine, absence, and the shifting feeling of an ordinary room.

On screen

The audience follows small daily gestures in an apartment where the rhythm of family life has subtly changed. Silence and spatial repetition carry more weight than overt dramatic events.

Direction / production

I co-directed the film with Kosan, translating a poem into scene structure and visual pacing while developing the performances and domestic atmosphere with the team.

Why this form

I kept the frame close to routine and the scale of the space modest. That restraint lets the viewer experience loneliness as a change in attention rather than as a declared theme.
