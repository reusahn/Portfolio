Experimental Documentary Film

Where is
My Cloud

Year 2025
Type Experimental Documentary
Runtime 22 min
Role Director
FILM / DIRECTORIAL CASE STUDYDOCUMENTARY / PERSONAL ARCHIVE

What remains when eight years of images disappear?

Where Is My Cloud is an experimental documentary beginning with the loss of a long-held digital archive. Moving through Korean landscapes, voice, and reconstructed recollection, the film separates memory from the platform that once stored it.

On screen

The viewer travels between lived places and imagined returns. Missing digital images are not simply replaced; their absence changes the film's voice, rhythm, and sense of time.

Direction / production

I directed the documentary, developing its personal narration, location-based imagery, associative editing, and formal movement between recorded and imagined memories.

Why this form

I chose not to treat technical data recovery as the story's resolution. The film instead asks how someone can construct a new narrative after a supposedly permanent digital record is lost.

Director's Statement

At night, I sang in the school studio and uploaded videos. People only talked about the singing. No one asked about my face.

But I am not a face. I am not an influencer. I am a voice. I am presence. I am feeling. I am conversation.

The cloud I've lost isn't just data. It's everything I see, everything I say, everything I feel. It's my perspective. My language. My way of being with others.

This project is the journey to find that cloud again. Or maybe, it's not about finding what was lost—but about creating something new from the beginning.

My art is the act of veiling love. Because I have been deeply loved, the love I hold within is vast. I peel away the old veils, only to carefully drape new ones over it again.

Through life and nature, through hardship and pain, the place I arrive at is always love. When all the veils are shattered, what remains—solid and magnificent—is love.

Perhaps love is the answer to every question in the world. Even my lost cloud may have been just another veil, broken for the sake of love.

Jonghoon Ahn / DOCUMENTARY / PERSONAL ARCHIVE
