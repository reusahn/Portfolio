Overview

Shininho is a real-time digital human project developed through a complete technical art pipeline from volumetric capture to Unreal Engine 5 deployment. As Technical Artist and Pipeline Lead, I helped transform high-density scan data into a riggable, animatable, and optimized character system for real-time cinematic presentation and exhibition playback.

The project required balancing emotional presence with technical stability. The digital human needed to preserve subtle gestures, facial expression, breathing, blinking, and body weight while remaining performant enough for repeated playback across multiple exhibition environments.

The artistic concept explores affection, grief, memory, and presence through a computational body. However, the production challenge was technical: building a reproducible pipeline that could support character preparation, animation playback, material response, Blueprint logic, optimization, and future iteration.

The pipeline was designed to be reproducible and scalable. Documentation and asset conventions were maintained throughout to support future iterations of the project.

Production Challenge

The project required a realistic digital human that could preserve the emotional presence of the original subject while remaining stable enough for real-time playback in exhibition environments. The main challenge was to translate high-density scan data into an optimized, riggable, and expressive character that could be animated, sequenced, and deployed across different hardware and display conditions.

The work needed to balance two goals at once: preserving subtle human details such as facial expression, breathing, blinking, and body weight, while also building a reliable technical pipeline for Unreal Engine 5 playback, iteration, and exhibition deployment.

Artistic Context

"The pipeline was built to carry something that no render setting can specify: the weight of someone who is no longer here."

Artistically, Shininho explores how affection, grief, and presence can be translated into computational form. Subtle gestures such as breathing, blinking, and weight shifts were treated not only as animation details, but as emotional traces that allow a digital body to carry memory and absence.

The technical pipeline was built to support this emotional goal. Rather than presenting the digital human as a static model, the project used real-time animation, camera timing, material response, and playback logic to create a living presence that could exist within exhibition space.

Deployment Context

The digital human pipeline was used across multiple exhibition contexts between 2024 and 2026, requiring adaptable real-time playback, venue-specific performance tuning, and reliable character presentation across different display and projection setups.
