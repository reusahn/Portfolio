Production Challenge

The project required a realistic digital human that could preserve the emotional presence of the original subject while remaining stable enough for real-time playback in exhibition environments. The main challenge was to translate high-density scan data into an optimized, riggable, and expressive character that could be animated, sequenced, and deployed across different hardware and display conditions.

The work needed to balance two goals at once: preserving subtle human details such as facial expression, breathing, blinking, and body weight, while also building a reliable technical pipeline for Unreal Engine 5 playback, iteration, and exhibition deployment.

My Role

As Technical Artist and Pipeline Lead, I owned the production pipeline between the raw digital human asset and its real-time presentation in Unreal Engine 5.

  • Built and maintained the digital human pipeline from scan cleanup to UE5 deployment
  • Prepared Maya rigging, UV organization, and FBX export workflows
  • Supported facial animation through blendshape preparation and MetaHuman-compatible integration
  • Set up UE5 Sequencer timelines for cinematic playback and character presentation
  • Built Blueprint-based interaction, trigger, loop, and playback logic
  • Refined skin, hair, fabric, and character materials for real-time rendering
  • Tuned LODs, texture streaming, material complexity, and playback stability
  • Prepared asset organization, naming conventions, and pipeline documentation for future iterations

Artistic Context

"The pipeline was built to carry something that no render setting can specify: the weight of someone who is no longer here."

Artistically, Shininho explores how affection, grief, and presence can be translated into computational form. Subtle gestures such as breathing, blinking, and weight shifts were treated not only as animation details, but as emotional traces that allow a digital body to carry memory and absence.

The technical pipeline was built to support this emotional goal. Rather than presenting the digital human as a static model, the project used real-time animation, camera timing, material response, and playback logic to create a living presence that could exist within exhibition space.

Deployment Context

The digital human pipeline was deployed across multiple international exhibition contexts between 2024 and 2026, requiring venue-specific performance tuning and reliable character presentation across different display and projection setups.
