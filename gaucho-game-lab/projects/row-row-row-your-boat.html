Row, Row, Row Your Boat

Production problem

The technical challenge was scale. With ten characters, small inconsistencies in identity setup, facial performance, head/body integration, materials, grooming, animation ranges, or timing could compound into a scene-level failure. I treated the cast as one production system while preserving each participant's individual identity.

How the ten-character pipeline worked

01 · Identity processing

Built each participant's MetaHuman identity from real-person source material and checked likeness, proportions, facial structure, and basic production readiness.

02 · Establish a character standard

Created a consistent QA target for skin, eyes, teeth, grooms, scale, and general presentation so the ten characters could coexist in one scene without obvious pipeline drift.

03 · Per-character correction

Resolved recurring look-development issues individually instead of applying one global fix where the source identities required different treatment.

04 · Facial performance ingest

Integrated Live Link Face performance per participant and checked the usable performance ranges before building the final timeline.

05 · Head / body assembly

Combined head and body animation, then corrected conflicts and artifacts that appeared when independently prepared performance components were evaluated together.

06 · Missing-range repair

Identified incomplete or mismatched animation ranges and repaired timing so the final character did not drop out or visibly break during the shared sequence.

07 · Multi-character timing

Arranged the ten performances as a canon-like sequence and checked overlaps, entrances, offsets, and synchronization at scene level.

08 · 5×2 staging

Validated the cast in the dense 5×2 composition, where framing and spacing had to remain readable even when many characters were active simultaneously.

09 · Lighting states

Prepared morning, sunset, and night presentations while keeping facial readability and character consistency across the full group.

10 · Installation delivery

Prepared and validated the synchronized two-channel Unreal output for final public installation at Melbourne Town Hall.

Why scale changed the workflow

A one-character fix is not enough in a ten-character scene. I had to distinguish between asset-specific problems and pipeline-level problems, then standardize the latter so corrections could be repeated reliably.

Technical issues resolved

  • Head / body conflicts
  • Missing facial performance ranges
  • Animation timing mismatches
  • Character-level visual inconsistencies
  • Dense multi-character staging
  • Lighting consistency across presentation states
This project shows production-scale technical art: the workflow had to remain predictable enough to repeat across ten people without reducing every character to the same generic treatment.
