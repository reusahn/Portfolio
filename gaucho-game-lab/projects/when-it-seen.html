When It Seen

A three-performer technical-animation pipeline combining MOVIN TRACIN body capture with Rokoko / iPhone facial performance, synchronized and validated in Unreal before full-character Alembic delivery.

Production problem

Body and face were captured through different systems. Each dancer therefore needed a controlled ingest, synchronization, in-engine validation, and final deformation bake before downstream cloth work could begin.

Capture-to-handoff process

01 · Organize performer takes

Separated and organized body material for each of the three dancers so the later face matching and final character assembly could be evaluated per performer.

02 · Body ingest

Brought MOVIN TRACIN body performance into the character pipeline and checked that the usable motion range matched the intended take.

03 · Facial ingest

Prepared the Rokoko / iPhone facial performances separately, preserving performer-specific expression rather than treating the face as a generic overlay.

04 · Timing alignment

Aligned independently captured body and facial streams by performance range and visible action, then corrected offsets before committing to a bake.

05 · Character assembly

Combined the synchronized body and facial components into complete animated characters for in-engine evaluation.

06 · Unreal validation

Reviewed timing, facial/body coherence, and full-character deformation in Unreal, where integration errors were easier to identify than in isolated source data.

07 · Repair and re-check

Corrected mismatches or problem ranges and replayed them in context until the complete character held together across the required shot range.

08 · Bake final deformation

Baked the approved character performance so downstream departments did not need to reconstruct the separate capture layers.

09 · Alembic export

Exported full-character Alembic deformation caches with the validated performance range for cross-DCC delivery.

10 · Houdini handoff

Delivered the caches for downstream Houdini cloth-contact work. The Houdini cloth simulation itself was handled by another collaborator and is not claimed as my contribution.

Why Alembic at the boundary

The handoff needed a dependable representation of the final animated deformation. Delivering the approved character as a cache prevented the downstream stage from having to reproduce the body/face assembly or retargeting decisions.

Technical animation responsibilities

  • Multi-performer capture organization
  • Body / face stream integration
  • Timing and range alignment
  • In-engine performance validation
  • Final deformation baking
  • Cross-DCC cache delivery
This case presents technical animation as production infrastructure: capture is only the first step. The useful result is synchronized, validated character motion that another department can consume reliably.
