Yaloo Multi-Age Digital Humans

A digital-human age-variation pipeline built around preserving one recognizable identity while producing child, adult, middle-aged, and elderly states for real-time presentation.

The technical goal was not simply to build four unrelated characters. The important problem was keeping the same person readable while changing body proportion, surface age, hair, and presentation.

How I built it

01 · Identity capture

Started from depth-video source material and built a MetaHuman-based identity that could act as the visual anchor for the later age variations.

02 · Base character validation

Checked the face, silhouette, skin response, eyes, hair, and overall likeness in a real-time lighting context before branching into age states.

03 · Child-body adaptation

Used a Character Creator body for the child version and reconciled the face and body presentation so the identity remained recognizable at a different proportion and scale.

04 · Middle-age lookdev

Developed the middle-aged state through controlled surface and material changes rather than treating it as a completely separate rebuild.

05 · Elderly lookdev

Extended the same approach for the elderly state, emphasizing age through material and surface response while retaining the underlying facial identity.

06 · Hair and presentation

Adjusted hair, lighting, and surrounding presentation per age state so the technical age changes remained legible in final real-time imagery.

07 · Engine integration

Validated the characters in Unity and Unreal production contexts, resolving differences in materials, lighting, animation integration, and final presentation.

08 · Iteration and delivery

Repeatedly compared the age states together, corrected continuity problems, and prepared the final characters for exhibition and cinematic outputs.

Technical decisions

Identity before aging

The first priority was a stable recognizable base. Age effects only work if the viewer can still read the same person underneath the changes.

Material-driven variation

Middle-aged and elderly states were developed through surface and material changes, allowing age to be iterated without rebuilding the complete character every time.

Cross-engine troubleshooting

Skin, hair, lighting, and animation can read differently between engines, so the work included engine-side adjustment rather than assuming a DCC result would transfer unchanged.

Long-running collaboration

The pipeline was designed to remain editable as the artist's project changed, making technical continuity and repeatable iteration as important as a single final render.
