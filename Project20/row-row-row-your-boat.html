OVERVIEW

Scaling one facial-capture and MetaHuman workflow across ten different people.

The performers were hired and recorded by the project artist using capture guidelines I provided. For each participant, the source package included iPhone Live Link Face depth capture for identity, a head-turn scan clip, a teeth-visible reference, and a separate singing performance recorded to a shared metronome. My work began when those assets arrived: I processed all ten identities, handled corrupted or insufficient source cases, tuned each character’s skin, eyes, teeth, and groom, rebuilt missing editorial beats in Sequencer, stabilized facial head motion against the body setup, and prepared the final two-channel installation renders.

02 / LOOKDEV & GROOM STABILITY

Per-Character Skin, Hair & Five-MetaHuman Overlap

The ensemble intentionally stacked five MetaHumans into nearly the same visual region. That made skin balance, bright hair, groom density, shadowing, and unnecessary simulation more consequential than in a single-character render.

SKIN / HAIR LOOKDEV — BEFORE / AFTER
FIVE-CHARACTER GROOM STABILITY
Task 02A / Skin Balance

Using both material response and additional key lighting when skin contrast became too large

A single lighting setup did not produce equally readable skin across all participants. When the visual difference became too strong, I adjusted roughness and, where necessary, introduced or changed key lighting rather than correcting everything with one global exposure.

Lighter faces could become overly bright while darker faces could lose readability in the same shared scene.

Roughness adjustment was one of the primary character-level corrections.

Added or modified key light contribution where material changes alone were not enough.

Keep different participants distinct without allowing skin response to break the shared ensemble image.

Task 02B / Groom Tuning by Hair Type

Reducing bright-groom response without flattening every character to the same settings

Blonde and light-colored grooms reflected more aggressively and could become visually noisy in the render. I pushed groom roughness toward a non-shiny response and reduced density on the brightest hair, while darker Asian hair generally stayed much closer to the MetaHuman defaults.

Raised toward a matte / non-shiny response when bright hair reflected too strongly.

Bright / blonde hair could be reduced to approximately 0.0001 where needed.

Used an approximate hair-width value around 0.0025 in the tuned groom setup.

Darker Asian grooms generally remained closer to the default settings.

Task 02C / Five-Character Render Stability

Keeping five overlapping MetaHumans at LOD 0 while simplifying unnecessary groom behavior

Each visual group contained five overlapping MetaHumans, so the render had to carry high-detail faces and hair simultaneously. I kept the characters at LOD 0 for the close facial presentation, used Deep Shadow on the grooms, kept hair / shader quality in the Epic range rather than forcing every cinematic setting to maximum, and disabled groom simulation because the characters did not need dynamic hair motion in this composition.

All five overlapping MetaHumans remained at LOD 0.

Used Deep Shadow across the groom setups.

Hair / shader quality stayed in an Epic-level range instead of maximizing every cinematic option under five-character overlap.

Disabled groom simulation because overlap made unnecessary motion / instability more costly and the shot did not require it.

Result

Five close-up digital humans could occupy the same composition with high-detail faces while groom behavior remained visually controlled and predictable.

CHAPTER OUTCOME

The lookdev system was not one universal preset: shared production settings handled the ensemble baseline, while roughness, density, key lighting, and groom response were adjusted where individual characters visibly broke that baseline.

03 / CANON-DRIVEN STAGING

Turning Vocal Entrances into Character Reveals

The musical round was translated into a visual accumulation system. Each singer lived under an Empty Actor, and the parent transform was used to reveal the character in time with the canon.

INTENTIONAL OVERLAPPING SILHOUETTES
Task 03A / Two Groups of Five

Organizing ten people as two dense five-character ensembles

The ten digital humans were divided into two groups of five. Within each group, the characters occupied intentionally close positions so the faces and silhouettes accumulated as new voices entered.

10 characters → two groups of 5.

Near-overlap was intentional and central to the visual structure.

Increasing vocal density became increasing facial / silhouette density.

The same compositional decision created the five-MetaHuman groom and render constraints handled in lookdev.

Task 03B / Empty-Actor Reveal

Collapsing and expanding the parent transform instead of building a new visibility system

Each MetaHuman was attached under an Empty Actor. I animated the parent’s X / Y scale so the character could remain visually collapsed inside the composition and expand outward when its vocal entrance arrived.

Task 03C / Canon Timing

Aligning the visual entrance directly to the recorded singer

Because the actual singing performance was preserved in the final audio, the reveal was aligned to the performer’s real vocal entrance rather than an abstract animation cue. Each additional voice therefore changed both the soundtrack and the visible composition.

Result

The canon became a shared audio-visual timing system: recorded voice, facial performance, and character reveal all referred to the same musical entrance.

CHAPTER OUTCOME

A simple parent-transform mechanism became an editorial tool because its timing was driven by the real structure of the round rather than by arbitrary character animation.

04 / FACIAL PERFORMANCE EDITORIAL

Recorded Singing → Missing Beats → Reconstructed Idle Performance

There was no separate body mocap pipeline in this project. The production animation came from the Live Link facial / head performance, and the main editorial problem was that the recorded singing takes did not cover every pause, intro, side-view transition, and ending required by the final installation edit.

RECORDED SINGING → EDITORIAL GAP → IDLE RECONSTRUCTION
SINGING KEYS → CLEAN IDLE TRANSITION
Task 04A / Shared Musical Capture

Using real singing audio recorded against the same metronome

Each participant sang the actual song while the iPhone was fixed on a tripod. A common metronome kept the musical timing consistent across performers, and the recorded singing audio itself remained part of the final work.

iPhone fixed on a tripod during the singing performance.

Shared metronome reference across participants.

The performers’ actual recorded singing voices were used rather than replacing them with synthetic lip sync.

Facial and head motion came from the same Live Link performance source.

Task 04B / Missing Editorial Beats

Building performance coverage that had not been captured for the final installation structure

The original capture plan did not account for every section required later in the edit: intro time before singing, pauses inside the round, transitions into the side view, and non-singing moments before the next entrance. I created those missing beats from the existing facial performance rather than leaving abrupt gaps.

Added usable non-singing time before the performer’s vocal entrance.

Created quiet bridge sections where the round required a performer to wait before singing again.

Extended the performance to support the front-to-side editorial change.

Created cleaner non-singing coverage rather than ending directly on an active singing pose.

Task 04C / Singing-to-Idle Reconstruction

Removing mouth-performance keys while preserving the small movements that keep a digital human alive

To create natural silent sections, I duplicated usable facial animation and removed the keys responsible for singing mouth shapes. I retained eye blinks and subtle head motion, then edited the timing so the character could wait naturally without appearing frozen or continuing to lip-sync.

Task 04D / Sequencer Curve Cleanup

Reducing residual vibration and unnecessary key density inside Unreal

After rebuilding the editorial timing, I cleaned the remaining curves directly in Unreal Sequencer. Gaussian smoothing and Smart Reduce were used where they improved stability without flattening the small eye and head movements that made the idle sections believable.

Unreal Sequencer curve / graph editing.

Used a Gaussian smoothing value around 0.1 in the cleanup pass.

Used Smart Reduce to simplify dense animation while retaining useful performance changes.

Filtering supported editorial cleanup; it did not replace the deliberate deletion / preservation of specific facial keys.

CHAPTER OUTCOME

The main animation work was editorial reconstruction: transforming one captured singing performance into a longer installation-ready facial performance with controlled silence, blinking, head motion, and transitions.

05 / HEAD / BODY STABILITY

Resolving Head-Motion Conflict Between Facial Animation and the Body Setup

Live Link facial performance carried head movement as well as facial deformation. When the head motion represented in the facial animation did not match the body-side head / IK motion, the character could visibly vibrate as the two systems disagreed.

LIVE LINK FACE — FACE + HEAD MOVEMENT
FACIAL HEAD MOTION → BODY CONTROL RIG
Task 05A / Conflict Diagnosis

Identifying vibration as a disagreement between two representations of head movement

Allowing head movement from the Live Link facial take introduced a second source of head motion. If the facial track and the body / head controls did not carry matching values, the result could read as rapid shaking or vibration rather than intentional performer movement.

Head movement contained in the facial / Live Link performance.

Head / IK-related motion represented on the body-side control setup.

Mismatched motion produced visible vibration / jitter around the head and neck.

The problem was not simply facial noise; it came from two motion representations disagreeing.

Task 05B / Head-Motion Transfer

Using the facial performance as the authoritative head-motion source

Because the facial recording contained the performer’s real head movement during singing, I copied the relevant head / IK-related motion from the facial performance into the body-side Control Rig setup so the two layers followed the same motion instead of competing with each other.

Task 05C / Final Canon Alignment

Keeping facial motion, head motion, real audio, and visual reveal on the same timing system

The final performance combined four timing layers: the recorded singing audio, facial deformation, stabilized head motion, and the Empty Actor reveal. The shared metronome and Sequencer editorial made those layers function as one canon performance.

Result

Ten independent recordings could be offset into a round without the head / body mismatch undermining the facial performance.

CHAPTER OUTCOME

The synchronization problem was not a conventional body-mocap retarget. It was a facial-performance integration problem: preserve authentic head motion from Live Link while making the body-side rig agree with it.

The Melbourne presentation used two square display panels and a looping two-channel output. Because different sequences were intended to read at different times of day, I rebuilt HDRI and three-point lighting rather than treating morning, sunset, and night as simple color grades.
