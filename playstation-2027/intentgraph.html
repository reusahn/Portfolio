01 / Scientific motivation

Movement is meaningful only in context.

A sudden displacement might be a collision bug, but it can also be a teleport ability, scripted knockback, a respawn, a moving platform or a camera cut. Detection that ignores game state risks flooding QA with legal action.

Research question: Does conditioning a transition predictor on player actions and explicit ability/control state reduce false-positive anomaly events under legal but rare mechanics, compared with unconditional novelty detection?

Secondary question: Can paired, deterministic replays with one controlled intervention support a within-simulator diagnosis of what changed, without claiming unrestricted causal explanations from observational video?

Three falsifiable hypotheses

  1. Action-conditioned next-state prediction has fewer boost/dash false alarms at matched recall than a state-only predictor.
  2. An explicit discrete ability flag improves prediction around legal teleports but should not mask illegal teleports.
  3. Paired replay under single controlled intervention yields more precise diagnosis than rank-ordered error magnitude alone.

A null or negative result is a valid finding and must remain visible.

Start with controlled physics. Add learning only where useful.

Prediction objective

Given a history of world states and player actions, predict a distribution of the next world-state components over a short fixed horizon. Evaluate position and velocity after normalizing units and masks for transitions in which player control is intentionally unavailable.

Training data must consist of appropriately partitioned valid transitions. Fault classes are held back for anomaly evaluation; otherwise the model may simply memorize injector signatures.

Architecture decisions to justify

  • Why a simple residual model is not sufficient
  • History length and prediction horizon
  • Teacher forcing / rollout drift
  • Handling missing or stale controller events
  • Normalization across movement speed and gravity
  • Using uncertainty vs fixed thresholds
  • Compute and interpretability trade-offs
03 / Controlled counterfactual experiment

Hold a replay constant. Change exactly one cause.

Paired replay example

  1. Normal: player activates dash at t=2.3 s; legal ability state is on; movement spike is valid.
  2. Intervention: replay same actions, initial state and seed, but disable dash authorization at the same instant.
  3. Fault: if movement spike still occurs without valid activation, log it as an injected ability-state violation.
  4. Compare: measure scores under D0, D1, D2 at the event onset and control for ordinary dash sequences.
  5. Audit: if the model learns merely to detect injected debug behavior, reject the setup and fix the protocol.

Necessary hard negatives

  • Valid dash at extreme speed
  • Forced knockback and stagger
  • Moving platform transports player
  • Cutscene/camera-only translation
  • Respawn transition
  • Pause/resume timing irregularities
  • Valid no-input drifting or sliding
  • Ability state changed legitimately during transitions

At least one entire mechanic family must be unseen during training and tuning.

Causal boundary: controlled modifications in the deterministic simulator permit statements about effects under that simulator's intervention. They do not establish causal blame for arbitrary bugs in third-party games or from RGB video alone.

Gate 1 · State replay

Verify fixed-step replay, timestamps, paired interventions and label correctness. Document nondeterminism if replay is not bit-exact.

Gate 2 · Predictive models

Train D0–D2 on held-out normal trajectories. Add consistent scoring and event aggregation.

Gate 3 · Explanation

Run matched interventions, ablations and unseen-mechanic tests; publish limitations with a concise demo and report.

A / B / C hiring-panel review

Questions that challenge the method.

Technical interviewer A

Is your transition predictor learning the normal game or recognizing fault-injection artifacts? Demonstrate the held-out split, action-history ablation, intervention controls and a case where residual magnitude misleads the diagnosis.

Research manager C

How would this generalize to a game with proprietary mechanics and no engine telemetry? Show which diagnostic features degrade, what a video-only fallback can do, and how uncertain explanations are communicated to QA.

Recruiter B — What makes this a coherent PhD research project?
