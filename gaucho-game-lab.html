01 / 3D GAUSSIAN SPLATTING · COMPRESSION / STREAMING

SplatStream Lab

Full-scene 3DGS systems study using pruning, FP16/INT8 quantization, compact payload accounting, and held-out CUDA evaluation. The selected 25% pruning + FP16 operating point reduced the measured payload from 185.43 MiB to 70.04 MiB while retaining 27.6234 dB PSNR, 0.9007 SSIM, and 0.1659 LPIPS.

Game / real-time direction: how can Gaussian or neural scene representations become practical, streamable representations for interactive environments rather than offline captures?

02 / INTERACTION-READY 4D HUMANS

Interactive Digital Humans

Baseline v1 probes locality in a real-time Gaussian-human representation by perturbing SMPL joints and measuring whether Gaussian motion remains inside the expected kinematic subtree. The current Seattle/frame-0 baseline contains 36 probe measurements over 472,958 human Gaussians.

PRODUCTION ↔ RESEARCH BRIDGE

The research direction is a continuation of technical art: controllable representations that can ship into interactive systems.

I am interested in a practical bridge between neural graphics and game-engine production, especially where representations must be editable, responsive, and robust enough for characters and environments.

NEAR-TERM QUESTIONS

Gaussian / neural assets inside a game loop.

  • Can Gaussian representations be compressed and streamed at rates suitable for interactive scenes?
  • Can 4D Gaussian humans expose local semantic controls that behave like production-friendly rig regions?
  • How do we bridge these representations with animation states, physics, shaders, collision, and gameplay logic?
  • What profiling and LOD policies are necessary before neural representations become practical production assets?
WHAT I BRING TO A LAB TEAM

Research that stays connected to production constraints.

I already work with the failure modes that appear after an asset reaches a real engine: retargeting mismatch, deformation errors, body/face sync, shader cost, asset handoff, runtime state logic, mobile constraints, and artist-engineer communication. Those constraints shape the research questions I want to pursue.
