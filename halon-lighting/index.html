01 / EnvironmentPlanned

Window / Practical ↗

Interior day–night re-lighting

A compact interior needs two convincing times of day while preserving the same geography, composition, and focal area.

What it will demonstrateThe day and night versions must change the room’s emotional reading without hiding weak lighting behind different cameras or set dressing.

02 / CharacterPlanned

Skin / Mixed Light ↗

Character lighting across skin and hair

Three distinct digital humans need to belong to one lighting language while retaining readable facial form, eye detail, and individual skin and groom response.

What it will demonstrateA setup that flatters one face may over-brighten another, bury the eyes, or make light-colored hair dominate the composition.
Scenario, shot plan & evidence →
03 / CinematicPlanned

Across the Cut ↗

Dialogue and reverse-angle continuity

A two-character conversation must feel like one location across a wide shot and two reverse angles.

What it will demonstrateMaking each face attractive independently can flip the apparent key direction, alter skin color between cuts, or introduce visible exposure and background changes.
Scenario, shot plan & evidence →
04 / CinematicPlanned

Light Through Motion ↗

Animated character and render continuity

A character turns, crosses a light boundary, and finishes a small performance beat while the lighting remains intentional.

What it will demonstrateA strong still frame can conceal lost eye detail, unstable groom highlights, heavy shadow changes, and first-frame render artifacts.
Scenario, shot plan & evidence →
05 / EnvironmentPlanned

Across the Threshold ↗

Interior–exterior exposure and navigation

A viewer moves from a shaded interior toward a bright exterior and returns without losing the room’s focal direction or the sense of changing brightness.

What it will demonstrateA fixed exposure may crush the interior or clip the exit, while poorly controlled adaptation can pump, flatten the contrast, or make the transition distracting.
Scenario, shot plan & evidence →
06 / EnvironmentPlanned

Wet Night / Reflected Light ↗

Night exterior, emissives, and reflection stability

A small night exterior needs convincing wet-surface reflections and a clear focal destination without becoming an indiscriminate field of saturated signs.

What it will demonstrateBright emissives, reflective materials, fog, and moving cameras can produce noisy results, confusing hierarchy, or an attractive still that breaks immediately in motion.

About / Jonghoon Ahn

Lighting · Film · Real-time production

I approach lighting through the relationship between people and the spaces they occupy.

My practice connects the visual decisions of filmmaking with the practical demands of real-time production. I have directed films, worked as gaffer on 정규민 〈닮은사람〉, and developed digital humans and interactive performances in Unreal Engine and Unity.

For this portfolio, the focus is Unreal Engine lighting: character readability, skin and hair response, environmental atmosphere, and the choices that connect a shot’s mood to its sources.
