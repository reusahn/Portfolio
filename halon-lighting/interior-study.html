01 / Visual intention

One room, two reasons for the light.

The study will use one compact room with a window, a practical lamp, and a small area of seating. Daylight will establish an open, quiet space. The night setup will let the practical become the focal source while retaining enough environmental light to describe the room.

Day / Window

Build a coherent daylight direction, soft interior bounce, and a restrained hierarchy around the seating area.

Night / Practical

Balance a warm practical source with cooler exterior ambience. Preserve shape in the shadows without flattening the scene.

Continuity

Keep the main camera positions and set dressing consistent between the two conditions. Use additional views to check that the room holds together beyond one frame.

03 / Planned workflow

Start with the lighting baseline, then shape the scene.

The scene will be developed in stages so each source has a clear purpose and each decision can be compared from a saved camera.

  1. Choose the room and establish the baseline.

    Use an existing environment with a usable window and practical fixtures. Record the asset source, inspect material response, and save two camera positions before rebuilding the lighting.

  2. Build global illumination and exposure.

    Establish the environment, the main daylight direction, and a controlled exposure setup. Review brightness and color before adding local support lights.

  3. Add motivated local sources.

    Use the window and practical fixtures as the visible logic for the lighting. Add support only where the camera, materials, or readability require it.

  4. Develop the second time condition.

    Keep the same room and matched cameras. Change the lighting hierarchy for night, then review the room from additional positions.

  5. Diagnose and document.

    Inspect relevant lighting views and GPU timings on the actual machine. Record the resolution, hardware, and rendering setup with any reported measurements.

06 / Production plan

Three days, one controlled environment.

The schedule assumes roughly four to six focused hours per day. If time tightens, the main matched camera pair takes priority over additional shots.

Day 1 / Daylight

Select the room, organize the sources, establish exposure and cameras, complete the daylight wide shot, and save the first breakdown captures.

Day 2 / Night

Build the practical-led night setup, refine material response, match both camera positions, and inspect the room from additional angles.

Day 3 / Delivery

Resolve artifacts, render the final frames and short motion, capture the lighting layout and diagnostics, then replace the planning material with results.

Completion criteria & credit wording
  • Two distinct lighting conditions with a readable focal hierarchy.
  • Matched cameras for the day / night comparison.
  • Visual continuity checked from more than one angle.
  • Original asset lighting excluded from any claimed re-lighting contribution.
  • No performance claim without a measurement and test configuration.
  • Final credit format: environment by the asset creator; lighting, cameras, and presentation by Jonghoon Ahn, limited to the work actually completed.
