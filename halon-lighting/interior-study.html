01 / Situation

The lighting problem

A compact interior needs two convincing times of day while preserving the same geography, composition, and focal area.

Constraint

The day and night versions must change the room’s emotional reading without hiding weak lighting behind different cameras or set dressing.

Visual intention

Daylight will feel open and quiet. At night, a practical lamp will become the visual anchor while exterior ambience retains the room’s depth.

Scene setup

One existing room with a window, seating, and a visible practical fixture. Save a wide and a medium camera before rebuilding the lighting; retain the same geometry for both conditions.

02 / Decisions

What will be changed, and why.

The work will be developed through controlled comparisons rather than a collection of unrelated looks.

Motivate the source

Let the window establish the daylight direction and the fixture explain the night key. Support lights will reinforce that logic rather than introduce competing directions.

Separate exposure from lighting

Keep exposure controlled while judging each setup. Document deliberate differences between day and night so a stronger result is not mistaken for a simple exposure change.

Preserve the room

Check the seating area, foreground, background, and reverse view. Keep local support restrained enough that the space remains coherent beyond the hero camera.

05 / Revision exercise

Respond to a precise art-direction note.

This is a proposed revision exercise for the study, not feedback from an employer or a completed client engagement.

Proposed note
The night frame feels atmospheric, but the seating area disappears.

Planned response

Retain the intended dark surround while selectively improving the focal region. Compare local-source shaping against raising global exposure, then document the chosen version.

Evidence to preserve

Keep the same camera or edited sequence, label the two versions, and write a short explanation of the visual change and its tradeoff.

06 / Technical review

Check the result under its intended viewing conditions.

Record the engine version and output mode used. Review real-time, headset, and offline-rendered material according to their actual delivery context.

  • Check for clipped practicals and lost shadow structure at the saved camera.
  • Inspect contact, indirect-light leaks, and surface response before adding more lights.
  • Review a short camera movement for reflection or temporal instability.

What a finished case will contain.
