Room A + two available characters

01 / Situation

Keep the room coherent across the cut.

A two-character conversation must feel like one location across a wide shot and two reverse angles.

Constraint

Making each face attractive independently can flip the apparent key direction, alter skin color between cuts, or introduce visible exposure and background changes.

Visual intention

Use a clear window or practical motivation, then support each camera with controlled shot-level lighting while preserving the scene’s shared lighting logic.

Scene setup

Stage two available characters in Room A. Save a wide, an over-the-shoulder toward character A, and the reverse toward character B. Use a simple shared performance or held poses for the first pass.

02 / Decisions

What will be changed, and why.

The work will be developed through controlled comparisons rather than a collection of unrelated looks.

Establish the shared scene first

Approve the location’s baseline before lighting the close-ups. Map the motivated source against both actors so direction remains understandable.

Use shot support with restraint

Where needed, organize camera-specific support in the relevant sequence or shot setup. Keep it from accidentally leaking into unrelated cameras or the general environment.

Judge the edit, not isolated stills

Compare the wide and reverses at the intended cut points. Review facial brightness, color, background separation, and highlight continuity in the edited sequence.

Planned comparisons

Change one decision. Keep the comparison readable.

These are planned versions, not completed tests. Each version will preserve a reviewable camera or sequence and a short record of the changes.

05 / Revision exercise

Respond to a precise art-direction note.

This is a proposed revision exercise for the study, not feedback from an employer or a completed client engagement.

Proposed note
The close-up of B feels like a different time of day from the wide.

Planned response

Compare the baseline environment, practical contribution, exposure, and shot support at the cut. Correct the mismatch and show the old and new versions in the same edit.

Evidence to preserve

Keep the same camera or edited sequence, label the two versions, and write a short explanation of the visual change and its tradeoff.

06 / Technical review

Check the result under its intended viewing conditions.

Record the engine version and output mode used. Review real-time, headset, and offline-rendered material according to their actual delivery context.

  • Watch the edit at normal speed for exposure or color jumps.
  • Confirm shot support is limited to the intended camera or sequence.
  • Check eyes, shoulder foregrounds, and silhouettes in both reverses.

What a finished case will contain.

The brief becomes a completed portfolio case when the visual results and supporting evidence are available.

  • The edited sequence communicates a consistent source motivation.
  • Each shot has an identified baseline and support contribution.
