Use the checkboxes to track presentation evidence. Notes are saved in this browser only; export a JSON copy to keep a portable record. Nothing entered here changes the published case pages or uploads a file.

01 / Production order

Build the smallest convincing submission first.

Eight lighting cases define the full coverage plan. The first submission should use the strongest completed work and a focused new result; the remaining studies can follow through shared sets and assets.

Wave 0 / Existing proof

Row + Jooan

Capture the missing role, source-isolation, motion/viewing-context, and delivery material around the work already produced.

Wave 1 / Character focus

Skin / Mixed Light

Use available MetaHuman assets to make a controlled portrait comparison and moving close-up. This directly supports a character-lighting discussion.

Wave 2 / Shared set

Room A studies

Build Window / Practical once, then reuse its geography for Across the Cut, Light Through Motion, and Across the Threshold.

Wave 3 / Additional range

Wet Night

Add the reflection-heavy exterior after the stronger character and continuity evidence is ready. Keep it compact and make the quality/cost comparison measurable.

02 / Review framework

Three perspectives. One evidence standard.

Weights total 100 points. Rate quality only after the actual outputs have been reviewed. A convincing brief does not replace a final image, moving test, or reproducible delivery.

A / 55 points

Lighting interviewer

“Show me how the image works, what motivated each source, and what happens between the selected frames.”

B / 15 points

Hiring reviewer

“Can I quickly identify the relevant work, your exact role, and the material that is actually complete?”

C / 30 points

Team lead

“Can I give you a note, get a clear revision, and reproduce the approved output without guessing?”

Common reasons to hold a case back from submission
  • A final still exists, but motion or the adjacent shot breaks the intended look.
  • Role, borrowed assets, or collaborators’ work cannot be distinguished.
  • A comparison changes the camera, exposure, and geometry together without explaining why.
  • A real-time or headset-performance claim is supported only by an offline render.
  • A revision is described, but the matching old and new outputs are missing.
  • Planned work is presented as a completed production.

04 / Interview structure

Explain one concrete decision.

Use this structure with the actual evidence. Do not memorize a result that has not been produced.

  1. Intent

    What should the viewer notice or feel in this scene?

  2. Reference

    Which direction, contrast, source quality, or color relationship guided the setup?

  3. Decision

    What did you change, and why was that change appropriate?

  4. Constraint

    Which camera, material, motion, viewing context, or delivery requirement limited the solution?

  5. Evidence

    Show the same-camera comparison, motion test, or capture that supports the decision.

  6. Revision

    Explain what changed after a precise note, the tradeoff, and how you checked the approved result.
