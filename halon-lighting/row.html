01 / Scope

Lighting within a ten-person character production.

The project artist recorded the participants using capture guidelines I supplied. I processed the ten identities, refined each character’s appearance, integrated facial performance, and prepared the final installation renders.

My contribution

MetaHuman identity processing, per-person look development, facial editorial, head/body stabilization, lighting, and render delivery.

Collaborative input

The project artist hired and recorded the performers and supplied the artistic context and source performances.

Lighting objective

Keep distinct people readable within an intentionally overlapping ensemble while changing the time-of-day atmosphere.

02 / Re-lighting

Change the environment and the character lights together.

Morning, sunset, and night used different HDRIs and different three-point lighting decisions. I rebuilt the scene’s lighting response for each condition, rather than treating the change as a single color grade.

Environment

Change the HDRI to establish the ambient light and time-of-day context.

Character

Rework the three-point lighting to shape the face within the new environment.

Review

Evaluate skin, hair, eyes, and overlapping facial forms together in the final composition.

03 / Ensemble readability

A shared setup still needs per-character decisions.

The same scene could over-brighten a lighter face while reducing readability on a darker face. I adjusted roughness and, where necessary, the key-light contribution instead of relying on one global exposure to solve every character.

Skin

Balance surface response and key lighting where a face breaks the ensemble baseline.

Grooms

Tune conspicuous light-colored hair and density so it does not dominate the composition.

Stability

Use shared production settings while disabling unneeded groom simulation for the fixed ensemble staging.

04 / Compositing & delivery

Separate the night background from the character render.

For the night sequence, I rendered the characters with alpha and assembled the background independently in Premiere. The final delivery used two square panels with front and side views, rather than a standard cinema frame.

Render

Characters exported with alpha for independent background assembly.

Composite
