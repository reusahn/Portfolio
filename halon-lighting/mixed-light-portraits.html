01 / Situation

The lighting problem

Three distinct digital humans need to belong to one lighting language while retaining readable facial form, eye detail, and individual skin and groom response.

Constraint

A setup that flatters one face may over-brighten another, bury the eyes, or make light-colored hair dominate the composition.

Visual intention

Build a neutral portrait baseline first, then introduce a warm practical and cooler environmental contribution without letting colored spill erase identity.

Scene setup

Select three available characters with different skin and hair appearance. Use one saved camera and consistent framing. Start with fixed baseline materials to isolate the effect of lighting.

02 / Decisions

What will be changed, and why.

The work will be developed through controlled comparisons rather than a collection of unrelated looks.

Build a shared baseline

Establish one neutral key and environment for all three characters before making exceptions. This makes per-character changes explainable.

Separate light and material corrections

Compare source size, direction, intensity, and exposure before changing roughness or skin parameters. Record any required material change separately.

Treat the eyes and groom as part of the face

Balance catchlights, scleral brightness, eyelash shadows, and groom highlights in the same final camera instead of approving each material in isolation.

05 / Revision exercise

Respond to a precise art-direction note.

This is a proposed revision exercise for the study, not feedback from an employer or a completed client engagement.

Proposed note
The warm side looks appealing, but one character loses facial structure and the blond groom pulls attention.

Planned response

Check source direction, highlight placement, and intensity before touching the character’s base appearance. Present the shared baseline and any individual exception side by side.

Evidence to preserve

Keep the same camera or edited sequence, label the two versions, and write a short explanation of the visual change and its tradeoff.

06 / Technical review

Check the result under its intended viewing conditions.

Record the engine version and output mode used. Review real-time, headset, and offline-rendered material according to their actual delivery context.

  • Review skin and eye highlights at final viewing size, not only while zoomed in.
  • Inspect the same movement and camera for each selected character.
  • Check shadowed facial planes and the relationship between hair and background.
