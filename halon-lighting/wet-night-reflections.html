01 / Situation

The lighting problem

A small night exterior needs convincing wet-surface reflections and a clear focal destination without becoming an indiscriminate field of saturated signs.

Constraint

Bright emissives, reflective materials, fog, and moving cameras can produce noisy results, confusing hierarchy, or an attractive still that breaks immediately in motion.

Visual intention

Use one dominant practical or sign as the visual anchor, with a restrained secondary color and wet-surface response that reinforces the route.

Scene setup

Use one compact exterior, a limited set of practical or emissive fixtures, and a short saved camera path. Avoid building a large city or adding rain simulation before the lighting and reflections work.

02 / Decisions

What will be changed, and why.

The work will be developed through controlled comparisons rather than a collection of unrelated looks.

Give the night one focal source

Build the visual hierarchy before adding extra color. Use darker regions to support the destination and keep the brightest reflections from competing with it.

Separate emissive appearance from illumination

Compare the appearance of the sign with the lighting it produces. Where support lights are needed, align their contribution with the visible fixture.

Test reflections through motion

Review roughness, screen-dependent behavior, and the selected reflection setup along the saved path. Explain quality changes using captures rather than a single polished still.

05 / Revision exercise

Respond to a precise art-direction note.

This is a proposed revision exercise for the study, not feedback from an employer or a completed client engagement.

Proposed note
The neon reads well, but the wet foreground steals attention and breaks up during the camera move.

Planned response

Compare roughness and source balance before raising rendering quality. Re-check the same motion, then show the revised hierarchy and any remaining reflection limitation.

Evidence to preserve

Keep the same camera or edited sequence, label the two versions, and write a short explanation of the visual change and its tradeoff.

06 / Technical review

Check the result under its intended viewing conditions.

Record the engine version and output mode used. Review real-time, headset, and offline-rendered material according to their actual delivery context.

  • Inspect the reflective foreground along the whole saved camera path.
  • Compare emissive-only and supported lighting contributions.
  • Record rendering mode, resolution, hardware, and relevant quality changes.

07 / Completion

What a finished case will contain.

The brief becomes a completed portfolio case when the visual results and supporting evidence are available.

  • The focal destination remains readable with and without the camera move.
  • The breakdown separates asset materials from the lighting contribution.
  • Any runtime or quality claim is backed by an actual comparison and settings.
