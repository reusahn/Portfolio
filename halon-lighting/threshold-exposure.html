Room A + a small adjoining exterior

01 / Situation

The lighting problem

A viewer moves from a shaded interior toward a bright exterior and returns without losing the room’s focal direction or the sense of changing brightness.

Constraint

A fixed exposure may crush the interior or clip the exit, while poorly controlled adaptation can pump, flatten the contrast, or make the transition distracting.

Visual intention

Preserve the sense of moving between two light environments while keeping the route and meaningful surfaces readable.

Scene setup

Build a short repeatable route through the room and doorway. Save a controlled cinematic version and an exposure-adapting walkthrough version so their goals can be compared explicitly.

02 / Decisions

What will be changed, and why.

The work will be developed through controlled comparisons rather than a collection of unrelated looks.

Choose exposure by viewing context

Use a fixed-camera baseline to understand the lighting, then evaluate an adaptation strategy for the moving route. Record the mode and relevant settings used for each output.

Guide with hierarchy

Use the opening, nearby surfaces, and restrained local sources to make the route legible rather than simply brightening every corner.

Check the return trip

Evaluate the exposure response in both directions and at pauses. A convincing outward move is insufficient if the room becomes unreadable when the viewer returns.

05 / Revision exercise

Respond to a precise art-direction note.

This is a proposed revision exercise for the study, not feedback from an employer or a completed client engagement.

Proposed note
The doorway is attractive, but entering the room causes a distracting brightness jump.

Planned response

Review adaptation and the contrast distribution along the same path. Compare the revised route at the same pauses and identify the remaining artistic compromise.

Evidence to preserve

Keep the same camera or edited sequence, label the two versions, and write a short explanation of the visual change and its tradeoff.

06 / Technical review

Check the result under its intended viewing conditions.

Record the engine version and output mode used. Review real-time, headset, and offline-rendered material according to their actual delivery context.

  • Run the route outward, backward, and with a pause at the doorway.
  • Check whether local exposure, if used, changes the intended highlight and shadow relationships.
  • Record measurements with a named hardware and rendering configuration.
