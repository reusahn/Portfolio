03 / NARRATIVE GAMEPLAY · PLAYER PERSPECTIVE · VR

NARCI + Be NARCI

Two companion VR experiences built around opposite roles: NARCI places the viewer inside repeated encounters with narcissistic figures, while Be NARCI reverses the perspective and gives the participant power over other human-like figures.

OVERVIEW

The mechanic changes when the player’s moral position changes.

NARCI and Be NARCI are useful technical-design evidence because they treat player perspective as part of the system. In the first work, the viewer occupies a confined elevator and encounters manipulative figures through a sequence designed to erode confidence before reaching self-confrontation and restoration. In the companion work, the participant is given the controlling role and can transform graceful human-like figures into statues or scrap-metal forms. The meaning is produced through what the player is allowed — or forced — to do.

CASE STUDY
01 / NARCI / SEQUENCE

Confinement, repeated encounters, and emotional progression

NARCI places the participant inside an elevator and brings them face-to-face with multiple narcissistic figures. The experience uses disorienting encounters and gaslighting-like dialogue to create a progressive emotional arc rather than a single isolated interaction.

SPACE

Elevator

A confined location reinforces repetition and lack of escape.

SEQUENCE

Multiple Figures

The emotional effect accumulates across encounters.

DIALOGUE

Manipulation

Conversation is part of the player-facing pressure.

RESOLUTION

Self-Confrontation

The sequence eventually opens toward restoration rather than ending at damage.

02 / BE NARCI / AGENCY

Role reversal turns theme into player action

Be NARCI is not simply a second telling of the same story. It changes the participant’s role. The player now holds power over free-moving human-like figures and can transform them into statues or scrap-metal forms. The action itself is the critical statement: control becomes something the participant performs.

PLAYER ROLE

Controller

The participant is moved from victim-side observation to active power.

TARGET

Human-like Figures

The player’s action is applied to characters rather than an abstract UI value.

VISIBLE STATE

Freedom → Fixed / Scrap

The consequence is legible as a strong visual state change.

DESIGN PURPOSE

Perspective Shift

The mechanic asks the player to experience the ethical problem from the opposite side.

03 / NARRATIVE PRINCIPLE

Convey emotion through rules, not only dialogue

The most transferable design lesson from the pair is that narrative meaning can be embedded in who has agency, what actions are available, how a space constrains the player, and what consequences are shown. That is directly relevant to narrative gameplay design: story and interaction can carry the same beat.

04 / PRODUCTION OWNERSHIP

Directing and technical implementation were part of the same task

My role on both works was Director and Technical Artist. That meant the intended psychological sequence had to remain coherent after it became a real-time VR experience — a practical bridge between narrative direction and implementation.

TAKEAWAY

Two VR works demonstrating that player role, available actions, encounter order, and spatial constraint can carry narrative meaning as directly as dialogue or cinematics.
