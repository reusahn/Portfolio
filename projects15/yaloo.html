What I owned

I built the practical age-variant character pipeline: processing the depth-video source into a MetaHuman identity, adapting a separate child body, developing the middle-aged and elderly surface states, and creating smooth material transitions between age variants.

The middle-aged and elderly versions were material and texture variations of the adult character, not separate reconstructed meshes. The child version used a CC4 child body combined with the MetaHuman head.

TASK 01 / IDENTITY

Depth Video → MetaHuman Identity

The first task was to establish a recognizable digital-human base from the available depth-video source without creating a custom character from scratch.

Problem

Create a recognizable real-time Yaloo identity from captured source data.

Decision

Use the depth-video capture as the source for MetaHuman Identity.

Implementation

Processed the captured facial data through MetaHuman Identity to establish the base face and production character framework.

Result

A consistent adult identity that could serve as the shared base for later age states.

IDENTITY DEVELOPMENT · SOURCE / DIGITAL HUMAN

Depth-video capture of the real subject.

Build one reliable adult identity first, then derive age variants from that shared identity.

A consistent facial base reduced unnecessary re-authoring across the different age states.

TASK 02 / PROPORTION

Child Body Adaptation

The child state could not be solved convincingly through surface aging alone because the body proportions needed to change. I kept the identity-bearing MetaHuman head and changed the body solution instead.

Problem

The adult body proportions did not read as a younger character.

Decision

Preserve the MetaHuman head while replacing the body with a CC4 child body.

Implementation

Integrated the CC4 child body with the MetaHuman head to create a younger silhouette and proportion set.

Result

A child variant that retained the recognizable facial identity while changing the body proportions.

The child version represents a structural change in body proportion. This is different from the middle-aged and elderly states, which intentionally reused the adult geometry and changed the surface presentation instead.
TASK 03 / AGE LOOKDEV

Middle-Aged & Elderly Surface States

For the older states, the goal was not to rebuild the mesh. The production decision was to preserve the adult character geometry and express aging through controlled texture and material variation.

Problem

Create clearly different age reads while maintaining a consistent character identity.

Decision

Keep the adult mesh and create age-specific texture/material states.

Implementation

Developed separate middle-aged and elderly material variants rather than reconstructing new geometry for each state.

Result

Three readable life stages with a shared facial base and lower character-management complexity.

The same recognizable facial identity remains the anchor across age states.

Only the child body required a structural body change; older variants remained material-driven.

This approach prioritizes fast, controllable age variation over separate high-cost character rebuilds.

TASK 04 / TRANSITION

Material-Driven Age Transition

The age states were designed to transition, not only to exist as separate still characters. I used smooth material fades to move between the middle-aged and elderly surface states without swapping geometry.

Problem

Hard switching between age states would make the transformation feel like an asset replacement.

Decision

Keep the geometry stable and transition the age-specific surface states through material blending.

Implementation

Prepared separate age materials and animated smooth fade in/out behavior between the states.

Result

A continuous visual aging transition without topology changes between the adult age variants.

DIGITAL-HUMAN AGE / MATERIAL STUDYVIMEO · AUTOPLAY · TIMELINE + VOLUME
Pipeline result

The final setup separates the age problem into two production strategies: structural adaptation for the child body, and surface/material adaptation for middle-aged and elderly states. That kept the identity consistent while avoiding unnecessary geometry rebuilds.

05 / INSTALLATION CONTEXT

Digital Human at Installation Scale

The final digital-human imagery was also presented at architectural scale. This context made facial silhouette, material response, and expression legibility visible beyond a close-up development viewport.

INSTALLATION-SCALE PRESENTATION
TAKEAWAY

A compact digital-human case focused on choosing the right level of intervention: rebuild proportions when age changes the body, reuse geometry when surface variation is enough, and keep the underlying identity consistent across the full age range.
