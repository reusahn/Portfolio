FastGS Training Efficiency

A two-scene study of how shorter optimization budgets and densification schedules affect 3D Gaussian Splatting training time, model growth, held-out quality, memory, and observed rendering throughput.

Question

How much can 3DGS training time be reduced through budget and densification-schedule changes before held-out quality degrades materially? Six Bonsai presets were used to identify candidate operating points, followed by a smaller Garden replication to test whether the behavior generalized across scene complexity.

Bonsai: about 29% less training time without a held-out quality loss.

The balanced 5K schedule reduced training time by about 28.95%, Gaussian/checkpoint scale by about 11.96%, and peak memory by about 14.39% relative to the 7K baseline, while PSNR increased by 0.58 dB. This is an empirical operating point for this scene, not a universal training recipe.

Consistent cost reduction

The balanced schedule again cut training time by about 30%. Model growth, checkpoint size, and memory also decreased substantially.

Scene-dependent quality cost

Unlike Bonsai, Garden paid a measurable quality penalty. The same schedule reduced PSNR by about 0.46 dB and increased LPIPS, demonstrating that an efficient schedule on one scene should not be assumed to transfer unchanged.

Research conclusion

A shorter 5K 3DGS schedule with early densification stop consistently reduced training cost across Bonsai and Garden, while the quality trade-off was scene dependent. The study uses a single run per configuration, so the results are descriptive rather than statistical. No significance claim is made.
