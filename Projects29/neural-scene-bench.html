NeuralScene Bench

A controlled comparison of 3D Gaussian Splatting and NeRF under the same Nerfstudio data parser, held-out split, image scale, and evaluation pipeline. The benchmark asks how representation choice changes reconstruction quality and observed evaluator throughput under a matched nominal 5K-iteration budget.

Protocol

Controlled inputs

Bonsai and Garden from Mip-NeRF 360. Both methods use the same COLMAP parser route, logical images path with explicit 2× downscale, interval held-out split, and evaluation interval 8.

Controlled camera treatment

Nerfacto defaults to camera optimization while Splatfacto defaults off in the pinned Nerfstudio version. The benchmark explicitly disables camera optimization for Nerfacto so representation comparison does not silently include different camera refinement behavior.

Backends

Splatfacto uses gsplat. Nerfacto uses tiny-cuda-nn compiled for A100 sm_80. The environment was repaired and pinned to avoid mixing host Python 3.13 binary-extension behavior with the benchmark runtime.

Metrics

Held-out PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, rays/sec, and evaluator FPS from ns-eval. FPS is treated as implementation-specific pipeline throughput, not as pure rasterizer speed.

Result: quality was consistent across scenes. Throughput was scene dependent.

Splatfacto achieved higher held-out reconstruction quality on both scenes. Nerfacto had slightly higher evaluator FPS on Bonsai, while Splatfacto had substantially higher evaluator FPS on Garden. The result argues for separating representation quality from backend and scene-specific throughput behavior.

What the benchmark does not claim

Matched 5K iterations are not matched compute, convergence, capacity, or wall-clock budget. Nerfacto is commonly trained for longer schedules, so these results are best read as a fixed nominal optimization-budget comparison. ns-eval FPS includes evaluator and model-specific execution paths and should not be interpreted as an absolute GS-vs-NeRF renderer benchmark. Checkpoints contain training state and are not deployable streaming payloads. The study currently covers two scenes, not a statistically representative scene distribution.
