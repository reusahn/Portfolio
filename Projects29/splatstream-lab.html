SplatStream Lab

An experimental pipeline for measuring how pruning and numerical precision affect full-scene 3D Gaussian Splatting payload size and held-out rendering quality. The project connects model representation decisions to transfer cost instead of treating trained scenes as fixed assets.

Research question

How much can a trained 3DGS scene be reduced through importance pruning and quantization before held-out image quality degrades sharply? The experiment uses a simple view-independent importance heuristic based on opacity and geometric mean scale, followed by precision reduction and a generic entropy-coding stage. The heuristic is intentionally labeled experimental rather than presented as a published compression algorithm.

Selected result: 2.65× smaller with useful retained quality.

The 25% prune + FP16 configuration reduced the entropy-coded scene from 185.43 MiB to 70.04 MiB while retaining 27.62 dB PSNR, 0.9007 SSIM, and 0.1659 LPIPS. More aggressive pruning and INT8 quantization produced a clear quality cliff, which is treated as negative evidence rather than hidden.

Interpretation boundary

This is a one-scene rate-distortion study. zlib is used only as a generic experimental entropy backend and is not presented as a production 3DGS codec. Network transfer estimates are illustrative. The experiment does not claim Netflix-scale streaming infrastructure, production codec performance, or rendering acceleration.
