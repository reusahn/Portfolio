Ray-Scene Acceleration

A from-scratch performance study of ray-triangle scene traversal. The project compares brute-force intersection, median BVH, binned SAH BVH, and a uniform-grid baseline while checking correctness and measuring the actual amount of geometric work performed per ray.

SAH improved both traversal work and wall time.

The binned SAH BVH reduced average triangle tests per ray from 99,972 to 12.8 and produced a 444× speedup over brute force in this clustered benchmark. The uniform-grid result is also useful negative evidence: an acceleration structure is not automatically effective when the scene distribution conflicts with its partitioning strategy.

Why this supports neural graphics work

Gaussian Splatting and neural rendering research often reaches a point where representation quality is only one half of the problem. The system still has to move through scene data efficiently. This project demonstrates low-level reasoning about spatial structures, traversal complexity, instrumentation, and performance validation rather than relying only on engine-level profiling.
