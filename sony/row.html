10 participants · Two-channel installation

Row, Row, Row Your Boat

Ten individually tuned MetaHumans became a singing ensemble. The production connected capture specifications, source recovery, per-person look development, facial editorial, and two-channel installation delivery.

RoleTechnical Artist
Period2026
Production10 participants · Two-channel installation
ToolsLive Link Face · MetaHuman · Control Rig · Sequencer

My contribution

I defined capture requirements, processed all ten MetaHuman identities, tuned skin, eyes, teeth, and grooms, assembled the canon timing, corrected head / body motion, and prepared final installation renders.

Production context

Artist Jung Hye-jeong hired and recorded the participants using my technical guidelines. I did not directly operate those participant capture sessions. The artwork’s composition and creative direction belonged to the artist.

01 / Production decision

Specify the input before processing ten people.

The source package separated identity capture from performance: iPhone depth data, head-turn and teeth-visible reference, and a singing take recorded to a shared metronome. I processed each participant through MetaHuman Identity and reviewed the result against the reference footage. When a Live Link source failed, supplemental Polycam head data provided a fallback.

Why this approach

Standardize the required source package while retaining a recovery path for unusable files.

02 / Production decision

Use a common baseline and individual look development.

The same lighting setup did not preserve every participant equally. I adjusted roughness, skin response, and key-light contribution per character, then tuned eyes, teeth, and groom response. Bright hair needed particular attention to density and specular noise.

Why this approach

Preserve the differences between participants while keeping the ensemble visually coherent.

03 / Production decision

Translate a musical round into character timing.

The ten characters were organized into two groups of five. Parent transforms controlled reveals as voices entered, while Sequencer carried the canon structure, entrances, exits, and facial performances. I rebuilt missing editorial beats instead of relying on the recorded source to provide every required transition.

Why this approach

Treat timing as an editable production system so visual accumulation follows the musical structure.

04 / Production decision

Resolve head motion and rendering in the final composition.

I aligned facial head motion with the body Control Rig and checked the result per character. Five overlapping MetaHumans created a demanding close-up composition. I retained high-detail faces, tuned groom shadows and quality, and disabled unnecessary hair simulation before preparing the two square-panel installation outputs.

Why this approach

Validate the overlapping ensemble, head / body integration, and final output framing together.
