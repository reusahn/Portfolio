01 / Production decision

Expose visual behavior through runtime controls.

A Perlin-driven vertex-displacement shader created cloud motion, while C# provided directional noise-flow controls. The shader and runtime logic were developed together so scene behavior could follow artistic direction.

Why this approach

Make the effect controllable from the scene rather than leaving all behavior embedded in shader constants.

02 / Production decision

Separate visual fields from interaction logic.

I developed a raymarched metaball field alongside C# contact and proximity logic. Keeping the visual surface and gameplay-side objects distinct allowed the effect to respond to scene events without treating the implicit rendered field as conventional mesh geometry.

Why this approach

Choose the representation for each responsibility: an implicit visual field for the image, runtime objects for interaction.

03 / Production decision

Preserve vertex identity for deterministic growth.

Topology-changing growth would break blendshape correspondence. I authored exactly four matching-topology states in Maya and animated the weights through Unity Timeline / Animation. The resulting growth was repeatable and directly editable.

Why this approach

Trade simulation freedom for stable topology and predictable art direction when the shot requires it.
