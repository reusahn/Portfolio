Digital Human Shininho

A real-person scan became a recognizable, animated MetaHuman through source cleanup, a rejected Unity prototype, likeness reconstruction, hybrid performance capture, and final Unreal assembly.

01 / Production decision

A working prototype exposed the wrong production goal.

The source arrived as a combined face-and-body scan. I separated the face in Maya, rebuilt symmetry, cleaned and reduced the geometry, and created an animated Unity prototype. Rigging, textures, hair, and playback worked, but the reduced character had lost too much of the subject’s age and likeness.

Why this approach

Return to the original high-resolution scan. A functional real-time rig was useful only if the person remained recognizable.

02 / Production decision

Preserve likeness through a new topology.

I used Wrap correspondence and local fitting to transfer the high-resolution source onto MetaHuman-compatible geometry. The original skin information remained the identity reference. I carried texture information through the rebuild, repaired missing scalp coverage, and adjusted eye position and appearance in Unreal.

Why this approach

Evaluate skin, eyes, and hair together under engine lighting; a successful topology transfer alone does not establish likeness.

03 / Production decision

Make capture decisions while the performer is on stage.

I placed markers, calibrated OptiTrack, established the T-pose and Motive skeleton solve, reviewed tracking, and directed takes. Low-to-floor movement produced severe tracking loss. Clear failures triggered a retake; localized residual errors continued to animation cleanup. MOVIN TRACIN provided complementary wrist, hand, and finger motion where the optical solve was weak.

Why this approach

Use live tracking quality to decide whether to retake. Select complementary motion by the failure it solves.

04 / Production decision

Reconstruct damaged motion, then combine the best performances.

I reviewed and filtered the full-body takes in MotionBuilder, rebuilt damaged sections in Maya with HumanIK and animation layers, and retargeted the result to MetaHuman. Facial performance was recorded separately with an iPhone on a Rokoko head rig and processed through MetaHuman Animator. A shared clap reference supported body / face alignment and take mixing in Sequencer.

Why this approach

Filter noise selectively. Tracking gaps and broken poses require manual reconstruction; body and face can come from different takes.

05 / Production decision

Deliver the performance inside the final character system.

I assembled and baked the body and face in Unreal, checked eye alignment and skin response, and carried the performance into the final real-time presentation. The later Jooan VR work extended the same character and motion pipeline into an immersive scene.

Why this approach

Judge the deliverable as a complete performance: likeness, contact, facial timing, materials, and engine playback.
