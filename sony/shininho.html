01 / likeness

Recover the person after a functional prototype lost likeness.

The original face and body arrived as one scan mesh. I extracted and prepared the face in Maya, rebuilt symmetry, cleaned and reduced the source, and tested a rigged, textured, animated Unity character.

The prototype proved the real-time loop but weakened the subject’s age and recognizable facial character. I returned to the high-resolution source and used Wrap correspondence to rebuild a MetaHuman-compatible production character.

Production decision

Preserve identity in the source before optimizing the character structure.

02 / surface and eyes

Carry source identity through topology, texture, and eye changes.

Wrap transferred the high-resolution source onto production topology while retaining scan-derived surface information. I repaired missing upper-head texture coverage and tuned the transferred age detail under engine lighting.

The default MetaHuman eye placement did not fit the scan-derived head naturally. I corrected eye position and appearance in Unreal and evaluated the face, eyes, skin, and hair together.

My character-production toolset for Shininho included Marmoset alongside Maya, Wrap, and MetaHuman. The final character and performance footage on this page show the Unreal Engine presentation.

Production decision

Check the transferred identity under the final real-time lighting, including the eye region.

03 / capture operation

Make tracking and retake decisions while the performer is on stage.

I placed optical markers, configured and calibrated OptiTrack, established the T-pose and Motive skeleton solve, directed takes, reviewed tracking, and exported FBX motion for cleanup.

Low-to-floor movement produced the most severe tracking failures. Visible failures triggered retakes. MOVIN TRACIN supplied complementary wrist / hand / finger motion where the optical solve was weak.

Facial performance was captured separately on an iPhone mounted to a Rokoko head rig and processed through MetaHuman Animator. A shared clap event supported later body / face pairing.

Production decision

Retake clear stage failures; retain independent takes so body and facial performances can be selected separately.

04 / motion reconstruction

Choose a correction method for the failure, then bake the performance.

MotionBuilder supported whole-take review, selective Butterworth / smoothing passes, Euler cleanup, key reduction, and Plot / Bake. Maya HumanIK, animation layers, and direct key editing handled localized reconstruction and performance assembly.

For damaged floor-motion segments, I used valid poses on either side of the failure, reconstructed intermediate keys, and refined interpolation. The body was retargeted to MetaHuman and assembled with independently selected facial takes in Sequencer.

Production decision

Filtering supports clean data. Missing tracking and broken poses need deliberate reconstruction.

05 / engine and vr delivery

Finish the character inside the presentation it will inhabit.

I assembled and baked the performance in Unreal and reviewed likeness, eye alignment, materials, body contact, and facial timing in the final character system.

The later Jooan VR extension connected the same character practice to immersive staging and hand-driven Niagara ribbon effects.

Production decision

Review the complete performance and scene together at delivery.
