01 / portrait reconstruction

Turn a painted likeness into a controllable character.

I reconstructed the face in ZBrush using the portrait’s pose, proportions, silhouette, and painted light as evidence. ZWrap carried the identity onto production geometry, and Maya supported the facial topology and UV work.

The portrait remained the likeness reference. Unseen anatomy was interpreted as production geometry.

Production decision

Establish likeness against the portrait view before preparing deformation and materials.

02 / custom facial system

Connect recorded coefficients to a custom expression library.

I built the 52 ARKit-compatible base targets and expanded the production library to approximately 256 mirrored, corrective, and combination targets.

Recorded iPhone coefficients were imported through an existing Python / Maya workflow. A channel-name case mismatch blocked the import; normalizing the source names restored the intended mapping.

Production decision

Resolve the capture-to-rig interface so the recorded signal reaches the correct expression channels.

03 / hair and garment

Use different representations for hair and clothing.

I authored the portrait-specific hair direction in XGen and converted it into card geometry for Unity. Facial hair used a separate secondary-motion treatment.

The Hanbok was simulated per shot in Marvelous Designer using the animated production character, exported as Alembic, and synchronized to the performance in Unity Timeline.

Production decision

Use real-time character hair assets and deterministic garment caches where each serves the presentation.

04 / hdrp look development

Adapt a proven rendering foundation to the custom face.

I adapted Unity’s digital-human HDRP material architecture around the reconstructed character, with custom maps, facial proportions, eye appearance, hair cards, and material parameters.

Skin subsurface response, roughness, detail, corneal highlights, hair response, HDRI, and scene lighting were reviewed as one character system.

Production decision

Judge the character-specific surfaces under the target lighting and viewing scale.

05 / public interaction

Deliver a complete museum application around the character.

I coded the Unity / C# interaction, camera presentation, UI, animation triggers, timing, and magnifier-style viewing behavior.

WebCamTexture supplied the visitor image for the on-screen composite. A touch interaction triggered a character V-pose, allowing visitors to frame and photograph the shared display.

Production decision

Close the pipeline with the actual visitor-facing system, including animation, camera, and interaction.
