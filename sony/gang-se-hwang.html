Custom facial system · Museum installation

Gang Se-hwang Digital Human

A historical portrait became an animated museum character through custom reconstruction, a capture-compatible facial rig, hair-card authoring, cached Hanbok simulation, HDRP look development, and C# kiosk interaction.

RoleTechnical Director
Period2022
ProductionCustom facial system · Museum installation
ToolsZBrush · ZWrap · Maya · Marvelous Designer · Unity HDRP

My contribution

I reconstructed the character, prepared production UVs and textures, built the facial blendshape library, integrated recorded facial coefficients, developed hair and garment delivery, adapted HDRP materials, and coded the museum kiosk interaction.

Production context

The portrait supported a reconstruction rather than an exact historical scan. Unseen anatomy was interpreted for production. Facial import used an existing Python / Maya workflow that I integrated and debugged; it was not a newly authored capture solver.

01 / Production decision

Turn one painted view into an animation-ready face.

I used the portrait’s head angle, proportions, silhouette, and painted light as reconstruction cues in ZBrush. ZWrap carried the likeness onto production geometry, and Maya supported detailed UV work and the structure needed for facial deformation.

Why this approach

Keep the painted view as the likeness reference while making the unseen geometry usable for animation.

02 / Production decision

Bridge iPhone coefficients to a custom facial rig.

I built the 52 ARKit-compatible base expressions and expanded the production library to approximately 256 mirrored, corrective, and combination targets. Recorded iPhone coefficients were imported through an existing Python-based Maya workflow. When import failed, I traced the problem to coefficient-name case mismatches and normalized the source names.

Why this approach

Fix the interface between capture data and the rig so the recorded performance can drive the intended channels.

03 / Production decision

Choose separate delivery strategies for hair and cloth.

I authored the hair in XGen and converted it into cards for Unity. The Hanbok required more convincing motion than the tested real-time cloth setup provided, so I simulated it per shot in Marvelous Designer and synchronized Alembic caches to the performance in Unity Timeline.

Why this approach

Use card-based hair and deterministic garment caches where they best serve the final presentation.

04 / Production decision

Complete the character as a public installation.

I adapted a Unity HDRP digital-human material workflow for the custom face, then built the C# presentation and kiosk interface. Camera framing, character playback, live camera compositing, and the public-facing interaction were part of the delivered system.

Why this approach

A character pipeline ends with a usable presentation, including playback and interaction in the target environment.
