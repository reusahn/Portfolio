01 — SELECTED RESEARCH-LED PROTOTYPE

AI ZOO

An AI-mediated being pleads for help while emotion parameters drive voice, motor movement, and light. The question is not what the machine feels, but how humans interpret what it does.

01 / CONCEPTUAL QUESTION

Can a machine's behavior become emotionally consequential without evidence that it feels anything?

AI ZOO began with a gap between computational function and human response. A language model can generate words and an animation system can produce a gesture, but people may perceive the combination as distress, vulnerability, or intention. I wanted to build a prototype in which that interpretive leap could be encountered directly—not merely explained in a paper or simulated on a monitor.

The installation presents an apparently confined digital human. A CCTV-like screen, recurring appeals for help, a transparent moving sphere, and an interface for audience input establish a relationship with unequal agency: the visitor can act, while the digital figure appears unable to escape. The scene raises a moral question even before any conversation occurs.

AI ZOO is not a sentience detector. It deliberately constructs the conditions under which artificial behavior can be interpreted as emotion. The creative proposition then becomes an engineering task: translate language and gesture into controllable, synchronized sound, movement, and light so the audience experiences an apparently changing emotional state.

RESEARCH PROPOSITIONEmotion is not a value stored inside the model and then faithfully read by an audience.

In this installation, apparent emotion is produced across a chain of expectation, symbolic signals, physical responses, and human interpretation. The same machine output can carry different meanings depending on its setting.

02 / EXPERIENCE DESIGN

How the audience encounters the system.

The first interaction is visual and physical. A captive-looking entity appears to call for help. Its voice and the independent movement of the transparent sphere establish an atmosphere in which an audience member may already feel concern or unease.

The visitor can intervene through text or gesture. Leap Motion detects hand movement, proximity sensing introduces an embodied distance parameter, and repeated contact or action can accumulate in the system's state. A response may be subtle or agitated, rather than a single preset animation for each button.

The model's output is not restricted to text. Emotion-related values contribute to how the sphere moves, how a synthetic voice sounds, and how lighting responds. That distributed expression matters: an identical statement can seem plaintive or threatening depending on the timing, voice, and mechanical movement around it.

The audience then interprets the result. A rapid irregular movement may be read as anger or pain; a slow fading response may feel like exhaustion. Those interpretations belong to the person, not to a proven conscious internal state of the machine.

The loop continues when the person's next action reflects the meaning they assigned to the previous response. That is the crucial difference between a passive AI performance and an interactive installation.

03 / PROCESS / IMPLEMENTATION

From the first proposition to a public-facing prototype.

  1. 01
    Identify a perceptual discrepancy

    Start from the observation that audiences react socially to responsive artifacts even when they understand the artifacts are computational.

  2. 02
    Choose an ethically charged spatial situation

    Place the digital human in an apparent enclosure so vulnerability becomes a part of the interface rather than an explanatory caption.

  3. 03
    Develop the embodied character

    Prepare the character in Character Creator and Maya for Unity real-time appearance and face animation.

  4. 04
    Create the initiating behavior

    Use repeated calls for help, prewritten messages, and movement to establish a scene before the visitor gives any input.

  5. 05
    Collect multiple audience signals

    Combine text, Leap Motion gesture/contact intensity, proximity data, and repeated actions as distinct inputs.

  6. 06
    Interpret language as affective parameters

    Use AI-mediated text processing to express a response through dimensions such as anger, sadness, fear, and despair.

  7. 07
    Accumulate interaction over time

    Let repeated signals influence the system's apparent state instead of treating every new gesture as a wholly unrelated event.

  8. 08
    Define a response vocabulary

    Assign qualitative movement profiles to affective dimensions: unstable shaking, sustained pulsing, or a reduction toward stillness.

  9. 09
    Convert data into physical output

    Map affective parameters to the three Arduino-driven servo motors controlling the transparent sphere.

  10. 10
    Translate state into sound and light

    Coordinate generated speech, voice characteristics, lighting and response timing as different channels of one apparent mood.

  11. 11
    Synchronize the environment

    Use Unity C# and Python integration to keep model output, sensors, audio, motors, and visual feedback working in one live installation.

  12. 12
    Return the effect to the audience

    Let the person react to the perceived state, closing the loop through further gestures, speech or withholding action.

05 / TECHNICAL PRACTICE

What had to be connected—and why.

The information model begins before the visitor presses anything. The enclosure, monitor and plea for help establish expectations. This framing explains why the same motor movement could appear threatening in one context and vulnerable in another. Design and computation are deliberately intertwined.

At the encoding stage, text is mapped into affective dimensions and gestures into contact or intensity signals. Repetition affects how the system presents its state over time. The project documentation uses an illustrative vector of anger, sadness, fear, and despair to make these dimensions legible. The vector is a control representation, not scientific evidence that an AI experiences those feelings.

The physical expression stage is explicit. Three servo motors vary the large sphere's movement. Faster and less regular motion can be associated with agitation; slower pulsing, with sadness; motion approaching stillness, with despair. ElevenLabs-based synthesized speech and lighting changes offer parallel perceptual channels.

Unity coordinates digital-human appearance, lip sync and interaction while Python and hardware-side control mediate other subsystems. This division allows language, sound, physical objects and real-time images to respond as one installation. The result is a complex interactive apparatus rather than a stand-alone language-model prompt.

The interpretive destination is intentionally different from Shannon's conventional destination of a received signal: the work asks what kind of perceived psychological reality occurs within the human participant. This extension is an artistic and research proposition, not a replacement for established information theory.

06 / ITERATION / JUDGMENT

The choices that define the prototype.

DECISION 01

Create vulnerability through the setting

An enclosure and a voice calling for help produce a question about responsibility before the spectator has seen the model's capabilities.

DECISION 02

Represent emotion as control data

A multidimensional affect vector gives speech, light and motion a shared state while avoiding a false assertion that the machine literally feels emotion.

DECISION 03

Use physical movement, not only an avatar

The motor-driven sphere turns an internal value into a shared physical event that visitors cannot dismiss as just another chat message.

DECISION 04

Make delay and intensity part of meaning

Response time, speed and amplitude have expressive roles; not all communication occurs in the words the model generates.

DECISION 05

Treat interpretation as the unresolved variable

The installation demonstrates a designed behavioral loop. Whether viewers truly attribute empathy or moral concern remains a possible subject for later controlled study.

07 / SIGNIFICANCE / NEXT QUESTION

What the built system makes possible.

THE EXPERIMENTAL CONTRIBUTION

AI ZOO offers an unusual demonstration of AI as an embodied behavioral system. A computational state becomes multiple synchronized sensory channels, and those channels create opportunities for human interpretation. The prototype makes the questions of agency, suffering and anthropomorphism graspable as an experience. It does not claim that emotion attribution was experimentally proven or that the digital being possesses consciousness.

A subsequent controlled study could manipulate response latency, intensity, reversibility and enclosure while holding the dialogue constant. Such a study would investigate which variables produce the apparent emotional effect; the completed installation itself remains a practice-based experiment.
