01 / THE IDEA

How does a technically functional digital human become recognizable as this particular person—not just as a generic realistic character?

Shininho is a digital-human project developed with artist Yaloo from the scanned likeness of her grandmother. The character has appeared across installations and performances. My work connects the human source to a usable production system: geometry, rigging, texture, motion, real-time rendering, and later immersive presentation.

02 / EXPERIENCE

What people see, do, and experience

The audience sees a familiar human identity carried into performances and virtual scenes. Facial nuance, body motion, lighting, and the context of an installation all affect whether the figure retains its personal character.

The work has moved across production contexts and presentation formats, requiring the digital human to remain visually and technically coherent under changing demands.

A later Unreal VR extension explores how a virtual human can inhabit a surrounding interactive environment rather than remain confined to one cinematic frame.

03 / MY CONTRIBUTION

From concept to implementation

I began with the source scan, cleaning and reshaping geometry for a character pipeline. I produced an initial Unity prototype to test whether the capture could be rigged, animated, and rendered in real time.

That proof of feasibility exposed a deeper failure: the early version worked technically but no longer conveyed the individual's likeness convincingly. I reworked the character using high-resolution source data, identity-preserving geometry transfer, MetaHuman structures, and revised look development.

My technical direction extended through motion-capture transfer and cleanup, Unreal integration, and presentation decisions including sequenced performance and real-time/VR staging. These production systems support the larger project authored by Yaloo.

05 / CREATIVE JUDGMENT

Why these choices mattered

Realism was not a sufficient acceptance test. An automatically rigged character can animate smoothly and still fail the social test of being recognized as the intended person.

I prioritized source-specific eyes, surface details, posture, and temporal behavior while retaining controllable character systems for later work. The production method was iterative because each improvement had to survive the next rendering or performance context.

WHAT THIS PROJECT MAKES TANGIBLE

Shininho is evidence of diagnosing and refining a prototype, not merely delivering a finished asset. The process makes a direct bridge from character engineering to research questions about identity, perception, and controllable human representation.
