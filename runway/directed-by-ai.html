04 — SELECTED RESEARCH-LED PROTOTYPE

Directed By AI

A film and installation built from the filmmaker's own face and voice, transformed into fictional family members. Generative technology becomes the mechanism of the story's identity and inheritance.

YEAR2024
MY ROLEDirector · Technical Artist
PROJECT CONTEXTCalifornia Institute of the Arts
TOOLS / MEDIUMDiffusion Models · Language Models · Voice Transformation · Film Direction
ORIGINAL PROJECT VIDEO · VIMEOVIDEO DEMONSTRATION / DIRECTED WORK
01 / QUESTION / PREMISE

Can one person's digital traces become several convincing fictional identities?

Directed By AI starts with a problem specific to generative media: AI can rearrange visual signs of identity, voice and kinship without sharing the life or relationships those signs ordinarily imply. Rather than generate an unrelated character, I used my own face and voice as source material for multiple fictional members of a family.

The film and installation center on a mother who fears passing cancer to her daughter, and the emotional distance that develops within that family. Those stakes are not technological spectacle. They give the artificial characters a dramatic reason to exist, while their shared source identity makes the relationships feel both intimate and uncanny.

The work was conceived as a collaboration between the director and AI-assisted development tools. Language models contributed to narrative exploration and dialogue revision; diffusion-based image-making allowed identities to be recomposed; transformed vocal performances enabled a single source voice to carry distinct character roles. I retained directorial responsibility for selection, form, rhythm and final presentation.

The question is not whether AI has genuinely become a co-author with human intention. The experiment tests what changes in a filmmaking workflow when writing, image generation and performance can be computationally reshaped at multiple stages of production.

02 / VIEWER EXPERIENCE

The technical premise is embedded in a story about inheritance.

In the film, the audience meets a mother and a sister who appear distinct but are mediated by the filmmaker's own facial data. The uncanny similarity is part of the character design, not an incidental artifact.

Across dialogue and image, the story deals with inheritance, fear and emotional estrangement. The technological premise carries meaning because the daughter's relation to the mother is already defined by the idea of something being passed from one generation to the next.

The installation presents moving portraits of the fictional mother and sister. The characters' visual identities derive from generative transformation and their dialogue from altered performances, turning the filmmaker's body into the raw material for several relationships.

The work is a directed, edited experience. The audience does not need to interact with a live model to encounter the research question about self-representation, artificial family ties and authorship.

03 / PRODUCTION DEVELOPMENT

Twelve decisions from the premise to the finished installation.

  1. 01
    Define the formal provocation

    Make one person's identity the source for several fictional human identities, creating a visible contradiction between data origin and character.

  2. 02
    Build a narrative conflict

    Center the plot on the fear of hereditary illness and emotional estrangement rather than making model generation itself the story.

  3. 03
    Develop characters through dialogue

    Use language-model assistance to explore story structure, relationships and possible lines, then review the narrative material critically.

  4. 04
    Select the source identity

    Use the filmmaker's own facial and vocal material so the transformation is an authored self-duplication experiment.

  5. 05
    Explore diffusion-based faces

    Generate visually distinct but related character imagery, adjusting the relationship between likeness and individuality.

  6. 06
    Evaluate generated variants

    Compare outputs for consistency with intended characters and the tone of the dramatic scene; do not treat the first image as a finished performance.

  7. 07
    Construct moving portraits

    Turn selected family representations into moving-image material for the installation component.

  8. 08
    Transform spoken performance

    Adapt the recorded source voice into character-specific vocal performances for the fictional mother and sister.

  9. 09
    Coordinate sound and image

    Bring faces, voice and dialogue into one apparent performance, where the viewer hears the characters as part of the story.

  10. 10
    Direct the film's sequence

    Use framing, timing and edited order to establish relationships and a comprehensible emotional progression.

  11. 11
    Translate into installation

    Present moving portraits as spatial media in parallel with the film, changing how visitors encounter the fictional family.

  12. 12
    Finalize authorship decisions

    Choose which AI-assisted results communicate the intended conflict, and separate the role of computational generation from directorial judgment.

05 / MEDIA / IMPLEMENTATION

AI-assisted writing, image transformation and vocal performance.

The experiment uses two interlocking forms of generative transformation. Language-model assistance was applied to narrative structure and dialogue development; diffusion-based workflows were used to generate and expand visual character identities. Those systems helped explore options but did not determine which options became the finished story.

The same human source data passed through several fictional identities. This is the central design mechanism: the viewer sees separate family members while the maker knows their representation descends from a common face and voice. The generated characters make an abstract argument about likeness and identity perceivable in the scene itself.

Voice transformation extended the approach beyond still image generation. I used transformed recordings of my own voice to perform different characters. This connected representation and performance: the synthetic family was not produced by imagery alone.

Moving portraits served the installation, while film editing and directorial selection organized the narrative. The completed work is therefore not a prompt collection or a demo reel of model outputs. Its main technical achievement lies in coordinating generative content with story, character continuity, transformed voice and a viewer-facing presentation.

The project does not claim that I trained a new diffusion foundation model or that the AI possessed artistic intention. Its research value comes from constructing a directed filmmaking process around emerging generative capabilities and testing what kinds of identity they can make visible.

06 / CRITICAL CHOICES

Why the film was not simply generated.

CHOICE 01

Use personal source data

The work's uncanny family is created by turning the maker's own visual and vocal identity into multiple fictional roles.

CHOICE 02

Keep illness as drama, not spectacle

The mother's fear of inheritance gives the family's artificial sameness a second, emotional meaning.

CHOICE 03

Treat language-model output as material

Generated dialogue and structural suggestions must still be evaluated as writing, not accepted as narrative authority.

CHOICE 04

Build continuity across mediums

An installation portrait and an edited film require different pacing, but must remain part of the same constructed family.

CHOICE 05

Retain responsibility for the cut

What is omitted, ordered, heard and seen in the final work is a directorial decision, independent of how much material AI can produce.

07 / SIGNIFICANCE

AI changes who can be represented—not who carries responsibility for the work.

THE EXPERIMENTAL CONTRIBUTION

Generation supplied multiple identities. Direction made those identities into a family.

Directed By AI demonstrates how emerging AI tools can shape narrative and cinematic form. It asks how identity, resemblance and authorship are perceived when computational representations carry intimate roles. The result is a finished film and installation, not a claim that the work empirically measured audience response.
