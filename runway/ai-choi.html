02 — SELECTED RESEARCH-LED PROTOTYPE

AI Choi Jung-hoon

I am a fan of JANNABI vocalist Choi Jung-hoon. I built an AI-mediated version of my favorite singer to ask what admiration becomes when the person I love listening to appears able to speak directly to me.

01 / THE QUESTION

If AI imitates my favorite singer, what will I feel as a fan?

What changes when the AI is not an anonymous assistant, but a version of someone whose voice and songs already matter deeply to me?

JANNABI's vocalist Choi Jung-hoon is one of the musicians I have personally admired as a fan. That attachment became the starting point for this project. I was curious about my own reaction to a specific kind of technological imitation: what if an AI could look like him, speak in a voice reminiscent of his, and respond when I addressed it? Would I feel excitement, affection, discomfort, disappointment, or some mixture that was difficult to name? This was a first-person artistic question before it became a technical specification.

Unlike a generic digital human, the performer already existed in my memory through songs, interviews, expressions, and voice. My admiration supplied an emotional reference against which an imitation could be judged. I therefore approached the prototype from two directions: building a believable real-time character, and noticing how the relationship between a fan and an artist changes when a mediated likeness can appear to reply.

The project converts that personal curiosity into a real-time encounter. I developed an animated character in Unity and connected speech transcription, GPT-based dialogue, ElevenLabs voice synthesis, blendshape facial movement, and Azure Kinect sensing. Each component asks a different question: is the face recognizable; does the voice evoke familiarity; is the timing believable; and does a generated reply change how close I feel to a figure I already admired? The system creates the conditions for those questions, rather than claiming to have measured a fan's emotional response.

DESIGN HYPOTHESISThe experiment begins with admiration, not photorealism.

Technical integration lets me put a personal feeling to the test: the distance between admiration for a real performer and an encounter with his artificial representation.

02 / EXPERIENCE DESIGN

The encounter begins with recognition.

The viewer first encounters a recognizable digital character, not an abstract model interface. The similarity of the face and voice activates associations that the viewer brings to the experience.

A visitor speaks to the figure. Speech is processed into text, a GPT-based conversational response is generated, and that response returns as synthesized audio. The character's face articulates the speech using animation data instead of displaying only a transcript.

Azure Kinect adds a different dimension: depth and body input allow the visitor to appear in the system as a tracked presence, including a point-cloud representation. Gestures and proximity can affect the relation between physical visitor and screen-based figure.

The result is deliberately unstable as an identity proposition: a coherent technical response can appear personal even though its words, voice, expression, and motion have been computationally assembled.

03 / PROTOTYPE DEVELOPMENT

Thirteen decisions between a portrait and a conversation.

  1. 01
    Frame the problem

    Begin with a personal question as a fan of JANNABI: how would I feel if an artificial Choi Jung-hoon could answer a question I asked?

  2. 02
    Collect reference imagery

    Select photographic reference that establishes the intended facial likeness.

  3. 03
    Construct a digital head

    Use Character Creator 4 Headshot as a starting point for a riggable human character.

  4. 04
    Refine character identity

    Adjust specific geometry and likeness using ZBrush rather than accepting automatic reconstruction unchanged.

  5. 05
    Prepare production geometry

    Use Maya for rigging, UV and character preparation so the model can perform.

  6. 06
    Import into a real-time world

    Bring the character into Unity with rendering and animation systems designed around responsive playback.

  7. 07
    Integrate incoming speech

    Convert a visitor's spoken question into the text consumed by the dialogue system.

  8. 08
    Set up conversational behavior

    Use the GPT API to generate replies through a persona-driven interaction layer.

  9. 09
    Produce spoken replies

    Connect ElevenLabs-generated voice output to the incoming model response.

  10. 10
    Translate audio into performance

    Use facial blendshapes and real-time lip synchronization so the generated speech has a visible performer.

  11. 11
    Control the response sequence

    Coordinate asynchronous stages through Unity coroutine-based sequencing, preserving turn-taking in the encounter.

  12. 12
    Read the visitor's body

    Integrate Azure Kinect tracking, gesture and depth input; include point-cloud visualization of the audience.

  13. 13
    Evaluate the assembled illusion

    Treat appearance, conversational timing, movement, voice, and audience context as one experience rather than independent demos.

05 / TECHNICAL PRACTICE

Building coherence across tools that do not naturally agree.

The character pipeline has two distinct requirements: recognizable form and animation-ready geometry. Starting from a portrait, I used Character Creator 4, ZBrush, and Maya to move from facial resemblance to a production character that could be rigged, textured, and presented in Unity.

Conversation is a second pipeline. The system processes audience speech, requests a GPT-based response, produces audio, and plays back animated articulation. Unity serves as the coordinator that makes several external capabilities feel like one responsive body.

Timing becomes a design material. A generated sentence, a completed voice file, and a face movement are not inherently synchronized. The project uses a coroutine-driven sequence to organize the interaction, showing why technical control over handoffs matters for a multimodal prototype.

Azure Kinect introduces a separate sensing stream. With tracked body and depth information, the work can acknowledge the visitor's physical position rather than restrict communication to verbal input. The point-cloud presentation makes that computational perception visible.

The combined system illustrates a central difficulty of embodied AI: high quality in an isolated model output does not guarantee convincing behavior once audio, timing, motion, likeness, and a physical audience coexist.

06 / ITERATION / JUDGMENT

What matters beyond a convincing screenshot.

CHOICE 01

Familiarity as an experimental condition

My own fandom was the reason for the subject. Small departures from the person I admired are meaningful precisely because I already have a vivid sense of his voice and behavior.

CHOICE 02

A fully controllable character

Rigging and facial blendshapes turn a resemblance into a responsive asset that can express new model outputs.

CHOICE 03

One response across multiple media

Voice, lip-sync, gaze and presentation timing should be evaluated as an exchange, not as individually impressive technologies.

CHOICE 04

A visitor who occupies space

Body sensing and point clouds introduce an embodied relation between the person and the synthetic figure.

CHOICE 05

Simulation is not personal identity

The figure is a mediated representation. The project neither asserts the real subject is present nor claims to train a new foundation model.

CHOICE 06

Research questions remain questions

Recognizability and perceived intimacy motivate the work; controlled perceptual outcomes would require a separate study.

07 / SIGNIFICANCE

A multimodal AI capability becomes an encounter.

WHAT THE PROTOTYPE MAKES TANGIBLE

My favorite singer becomes an interactive representation—but the relationship I bring to that figure cannot be generated by code.

The prototype transformed a fan's subjective curiosity into a multimodal production experiment. I wanted to learn what happens when familiarity, affection and computational imitation meet in one embodied interface. It demonstrates integration of language, voice, facial performance, and sensing, while leaving the emotional question deliberately open. A future study might separate vocal likeness, timing and responsiveness to test their influence; no such measured outcome is claimed for this project.
