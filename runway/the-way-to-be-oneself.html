The Way To Be Oneself

A film made with a synthetic representation of its maker. The project examines the difference between an image that appears to document a performance and the act that was actually recorded.

YEAR2025
MY ROLEDirector · Artist
PROJECT CONTEXTIndependent experimental film

01 / QUESTION / FILMIC EXPERIMENT

When does a synthetic image begin to function as evidence?

The Way To Be Oneself is an experimental film using ComfyUI and generative-image workflows to produce a synthetic representation of the filmmaker. A generated face can appear to perform an action that the represented person did not physically perform for a camera. The work begins at that separation between image and event.

AI image generation makes it increasingly easy to produce a plausible frame. Filmmaking poses a different problem: how can a series of outputs become a continuous, authored experience with a recognizable voice, visual rhythm, and meaningful progression? If any number of images are available, deciding which should exist in the film becomes more important, not less.

I treated generation as a method for proposing visual possibilities, not a substitute for composition and editorial judgment. The work requires deciding on a premise, constructing and reviewing synthetic representations, rejecting ineffective outputs, refining an intended direction, and ordering the remaining images in time.

The project therefore makes an AI capability accessible through cinema: the viewer encounters a performed identity while the production process reveals that the apparent evidence of performance was mediated by a generative system.

THE CENTRAL DISTINCTIONAn image of a performance is not proof that the performance happened.

The film uses a constructed likeness to make that separation visible. Generation produces alternatives; the director decides what becomes the film.

02 / VIEWER EXPERIENCE

A film with an apparently performed identity.

The viewer watches a completed moving-image sequence rather than browsing a set of unrelated visual variations. Coherence is created through selection and temporal arrangement. A generated image only becomes part of the film when its appearance, framing and relationship to adjacent images serve the intended experience.

The presence of a synthetic version of the filmmaker introduces a perceptual tension. The image can look like testimony to a recorded action, even when that specific represented action did not happen as an ordinary camera performance.

This is why the film belongs next to my real-time digital human work. Both deal with the gap between technical reconstruction and a person's perceived identity; one does so through embodied real-time response, the other through directed moving images.

Unlike Escape Metro or AI ZOO, the final audience experience is not an interactive software system. Its prototyping evidence lies in the visual experiments, revision process, and successful communication of a question through a finished film.

03 / IMAGE-MAKING AS EXPERIMENT

Eleven stages of generation, evaluation and cinematic decision.

  1. 01
    Identify the representational question

    Begin with the idea that a convincing image can depict a performance that did not occur in front of the camera.

  2. 02
    Define the film's intention

    Decide what the work should communicate about the self before treating generated images as available content.

  3. 03
    Develop a synthetic appearance

    Use generative-image tools and a ComfyUI-based workflow to explore the look of a mediated self-representation.

  4. 04
    Generate controlled alternatives

    Create visual possibilities that can be compared rather than assuming the first usable result is final.

  5. 05
    Evaluate recognizability

    Consider whether the resulting identity remains legible and how the visual transformation changes its meaning.

  6. 06
    Compare framing and mood

    Review composition, light and visual tone for coherence with the planned sequence.

  7. 07
    Reject weak or inconsistent outputs

    Exclude material that may be individually attractive but would undermine continuity or the directorial premise.

  8. 08
    Refine promising directions

    Repeat generation and selection around visual approaches that can support the moving-image work.

  9. 09
    Determine the sequence

    Organize chosen material to establish pacing, contrast and a progression across images.

  10. 10
    Build continuity through editing

    Make adjacent shots belong to the same film through decisions about order, duration and visual connection.

  11. 11
    Finalize the authored film

    Deliver the work as a completed cinematic experience, not a gallery of model samples.

05 / DESIGN / PRODUCTION

What is being tested in this moving-image practice.

The technical system produces variations in form, style and identity. The directorial work is to decide which variations communicate the film's premise, which break it, and how the remaining material should be ordered.

I use ComfyUI within a larger filmmaking workflow. Its output is assessed against visual continuity, intended meaning and cinematic tone rather than novelty alone. Selection, refinement and sequencing are the stages where the film becomes authored.

The central concern is machine-mediated self-representation: a face can remain convincing without documenting the precise act it portrays. The film provides a concrete way to discuss generated visual evidence, something that also matters to synthetic humans, interactive worlds and future video systems.

No claim is made that the project trained a generative model from scratch, invented a new video architecture or conducted an audience experiment. Its technical and creative value is the disciplined application of generative tools to a finished artistic research question.

06 / ITERATION / AUTHORSHIP

The decisions that remain human.

JUDGMENT 01

A plausible frame is only a possibility

Image generation solves the creation of candidates. It does not decide whether a shot is necessary for the film.

JUDGMENT 02

Recognition can conflict with evidence

The more convincing a synthetic self-image becomes, the more its apparent performance can be mistaken for documentation.

JUDGMENT 03

Continuity requires rejection

Keeping only the most visually striking generations may produce an incoherent sequence. Editorial selection is part of the research method.

JUDGMENT 04

Cinema gives the experiment a duration

A still image raises a question about likeness. A moving-image sequence adds timing, expectation and perceived action.

JUDGMENT 05

Do not claim an unbuilt system

This is an authored generative film made with ComfyUI and associated tools. It is not a trained world model or a live interactive character.

07 / SIGNIFICANCE

A model can make images. A prototype can reveal what those images change.

EXPERIMENTAL CONTRIBUTION

The work turns generated self-representation into a question about identity, performed action and credibility.

By taking a generative workflow all the way to a directed film, the project demonstrates visual experimentation and narrative communication. It complements software-driven prototypes with the capacity to shape a new technology into a coherent cinematic experience.
