The Way To Be Oneself

A film made with a synthetic representation of its maker. The project examines the difference between an image that appears to document a performance and the act that was actually recorded.

MY ROLEDirector · Artist

01 / EXPERIMENT / POETIC PREMISE

Can a poem be written with images we cannot predict?

A human writes a poem. A generative system proposes moving images that cannot be entirely anticipated. What happens when they are composed into one cinematic work?

What if I could write poetry with generative video? The Way To Be Oneself began with a collision between two kinds of imagination: experimental poetry written by a person and moving images produced by a generative system whose results cannot always be predicted or fully specified. Rather than asking AI to illustrate a finished poem literally, I wanted to discover what would emerge when those two practices occupied the same film.

A poem does not necessarily describe a sequence of objects that should be photographed. It can move through association, rhythm, repetition, silence and images that resist a single interpretation. Generative video has a different kind of indeterminacy: the intended prompt or reference is only part of what ultimately appears. Unexpected motion, transformations, visual continuity and failure can all push against what the author initially imagined.

That difference became the material of the experiment. The human-written poem carries one line of intention; generated moving images introduce another line of visual possibilities. I was interested in whether their combination could produce a cinematic language that neither the text nor the model output would have created alone.

The film therefore treats AI as a collaborator in visual unpredictability, not as the writer of the human poem and not as a replacement for editorial judgment. I determine how the language and image are placed together, which outcomes are kept, and how the resulting sequence unfolds through time.

THE TWO MATERIALSHuman intention × generative unpredictability

The work is not the automation of poetry. It is an encounter between a human's experimental verse and machine-generated moving images, composed through cinema.

02 / VIEWER EXPERIENCE

The poem and the video do not have to say the same thing.

The audience encounters a moving-image poem rather than a conventional demonstration of an AI video model. Language establishes an emotional and associative direction, while the images may answer, complicate or contradict the words.

Some generated images will feel related to the poem through texture, movement or atmosphere even when they do not depict its nouns literally. That gap creates the possibility of an unexpected third meaning: what a viewer reads in the relation between word and image.

Because moving images exist in time, pace becomes essential. A phrase can arrive before the image that alters it, or an image can remain unresolved after the language has moved on. Editing creates the meeting point between human poetic rhythm and machine-generated visual behavior.

The film's experimental form asks the viewer to participate in interpretation. Instead of receiving an illustration that confirms one reading of the poem, viewers encounter associations that are less predictable and more open.

03 / FROM IDEA TO PRACTICE

Twelve stages of writing, generation, observation and editing.

  1. 01
    Begin with human language

    Write or select experimental poetic material as an intentional human act. The text establishes imagery, rhythm and emotional tension without dictating every frame.

  2. 02
    Decide to work through difference

    Treat generative video as a process capable of unexpected imagery, not simply as a tool that reproduces the language faithfully.

  3. 03
    Identify poetic motifs

    Locate images, tensions, moods and shifts in the poem that can serve as starting points for visual experiments.

  4. 04
    Explore moving-image possibilities

    Use generative-image/video workflows, including ComfyUI-based experimentation, to develop candidate visual directions.

  5. 05
    Leave room for unplanned outcomes

    Allow the system's interpretation of prompts or inputs to introduce shapes, textures and movements the original text did not prescribe.

  6. 06
    Observe the divergence

    Compare the imagined scene with what the generative process actually produces. Treat the discrepancy as information, not automatically as an error.

  7. 07
    Keep or reject with intent

    Choose results based on poetic and cinematic value, even when that value differs from literal fidelity to the words.

  8. 08
    Establish a conversation between word and image

    Position poetic lines and selected moving images so they respond to, interrupt or reframe one another.

  9. 09
    Develop rhythmic structure

    Use repetitions, duration, silence and changes of visual density to build pacing related to the language.

  10. 10
    Refine continuity without flattening surprise

    Organize the film so it has an intentional progression while preserving the strangeness that made the generative experiments worthwhile.

  11. 11
    Evaluate the emerging third meaning

    Consider what the combination evokes that was present in neither the poem alone nor the raw generated clips.

  12. 12
    Complete the moving-image poem

    Make an authored film from uncertain visual material through selection, editing and the temporal relationship between language and image.

05 / METHOD / TECHNICAL EXPERIMENT

How unpredictable imagery becomes poetic material.

The workflow begins with human-authored experimental poetry. The poem provides imagery and a temporal sensibility, but the visual system does not function as a dictionary mapping every word to one exact shot. I use language as the starting material for exploration.

Generative imagery introduces another layer of possibility. With tools including ComfyUI, images and moving sequences can be explored, varied and recombined. The significance is not a hidden claim that the system is random in a strict mathematical sense; it is that its specific visual decisions and transformations cannot be exhaustively predicted from the author's initial intention.

Iteration becomes a method of inquiry. I compare imagined and generated outcomes, look for resonant mismatches, discard outputs that do not contribute, and retain some that reveal associations I would not have designed in advance. The value of the process lies partly in unexpected material that survives the critical selection stage.

Editing then establishes a relation between two independent rhythms: the human poem's linguistic rhythm and the generated moving images' temporal rhythm. The two can agree, diverge or interrupt one another. Through duration, juxtaposition and sequence, material produced through prediction becomes a new form of authored expression.

This is why the work is not described as an AI-written poem or as an autonomous video system. The poem is human-authored and the film is artistically assembled; generative models contribute unpredictable visual material. The experiment concerns the creative relation between them.

06 / CREATIVE JUDGMENT

The selection of a surprise is still an authored decision.

CHOICE 01

Do not make a literal illustrated poem

The images should be allowed to depart from the text. Those departures can produce meanings unavailable to literal visualization.

CHOICE 02

Keep the poem's human authorship clear

The work compares deliberate poetic language with generated visual behavior instead of blurring the origin of both.

CHOICE 03

Welcome productive unpredictability

A surprising output is not automatically useful, but it can challenge the author's first interpretation and suggest a new direction.

CHOICE 04

Use editing as the meeting place

The poetic and generative processes become one film only through deliberate timing, repetition, contrast and juxtaposition.

CHOICE 05

Retain some ambiguity

Over-explaining the relation between text and image would eliminate the experience the experiment aims to create.

CHOICE 06

Show the experiment through the film

The completed moving-image poem makes a procedural artistic question perceptible without claiming to have developed a new video model.

07 / MEANING / SIGNIFICANCE

A film that neither the poet nor the model could have fully predicted alone.

THE CONTRIBUTION

The unexpected image is not a mistake to eliminate. It can become the next line of a poem.

The Way To Be Oneself experiments with the interaction between a human-written experimental poem and generated moving-image material. Its research value lies in testing how poetic language, machine prediction and directorial editing can produce a visual form that is open to discovery. The final work remains a human-directed film, not a claim that AI automatically understands poetry or generated every creative choice.
