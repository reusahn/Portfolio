CAPTURE PLAYBOOK

I think in scene types before I think in software.

For capture, the first question is not “Which camera should I use?” It is “What information must this moment communicate?” An entrance scene needs anticipation and reveal. Dialogue needs eyelines and editable coverage. A close-up needs facial readability. Dance needs body continuity. Action needs geography before impact. The camera strategy changes with the scene.

IMPORTANT / SCOPE

The scene types below are my capture framework. Where I have direct project evidence, I link it as proof. Dialogue and combat are presented as coverage methodology, not as claims that I have already shipped dedicated Call of Duty dialogue or combat capture.

01 / ENTRANCE · REVEAL

Make the audience notice the character before giving them everything.

Introduction, reveal, hero arrival, silhouette, first-look marketing beat.

BEST CURRENT PROOF
Row · Shininho/Jooan · Yaloo

An entrance is about controlling when information arrives. I would normally establish the space first, then let movement, light, silhouette, or framing pull attention toward the character. The reveal should feel motivated rather than simply cutting to a centered beauty frame.

FramingWide / medium-wide first, then tighten after the entrance reads.
MovementSlow push, lateral reveal, motivated follow, or static frame with subject entering.
LightingUse separation, silhouette, rim, or environmental contrast to announce the subject.
Edit NeedOne readable reveal plus optional closer hero angle for marketing flexibility.
Coverage

Establishing geography → entrance / reveal master → medium hero angle → close detail if needed → reaction or environmental response.

02 / DIALOGUE · CONVERSATION

Protect eyelines and editorial options.

Two-shot, OTS, singles, reaction, listening shot, conversational rhythm.

METHOD FRAMEWORK
Film directing background

Dialogue capture is not just alternating faces. The master establishes physical and emotional relationship; singles isolate point of view; reactions often carry more information than the speaking line. I would protect screen direction and eyelines first, then build coverage that gives editorial flexibility without flattening every beat into identical OTS shots.

FramingMaster two-shot → OTS pair → clean singles → reaction inserts.
MovementUsually restrained unless a story beat motivates a push, orbit, or reposition.
EyelineMaintain screen direction and consistent eyeline logic across coverage.
Edit NeedProtect listening / reaction shots so the edit is not trapped by dialogue delivery.
Coverage

Master → two-shot → OTS A → OTS B → clean single A → clean single B → reactions / hands / object inserts as needed.

03 / FACE · CLOSE-UP · REACTION

The face becomes the environment.

Emotion, reaction, eyes, facial animation, subtle character performance.

BEST CURRENT PROOF
Gang Se-hwang · Shininho · Row

A close-up succeeds or fails on small details: eye direction, facial animation, skin response, hair overlap, background distraction, and timing. The closer the shot, the less tolerance there is for technical noise. I treat the close-up as both a performance choice and a technical stress test.

FramingEyes / expression first. Avoid unnecessary headroom or background competition.
MovementStatic or very subtle push unless movement serves the emotional beat.
LightingPreserve eye readability, skin form, and facial separation without flattening the face.
TechnicalCheck facial bake, eye behavior, groom, aliasing, shadowing, and deformation.
Coverage

Medium context → medium close-up → close-up → reaction hold → optional eye / hand / prop insert.

Gang Se-hwang — custom facial systemShininho — body/face integrationRow — 10 facial performances
04 / PERFORMANCE · DANCE

Give the body enough space to communicate.

Dance, gesture, choreography, full-body motion, rhythm.

DIRECT PROOF
Shininho/Jooan · When It Seen

For dance or expressive body performance, an overly tight frame destroys information. The viewer needs feet, hands, rhythm, direction changes, and spatial relationship. Camera movement should support the choreography, not compete with it.

FramingFull body / medium-wide as the default; tighter shots become accents.
MovementTrack or drift only when it preserves motion clarity; avoid fighting the choreography.
TimingRespect rhythmic phrases and let movement complete before cutting.
ReviewCheck whether camera motion steals attention or creates VR discomfort.
Coverage

Full-body master → alternate full-body angle → medium gesture shot → detail / hand / face accent → ending hold.

Shininho/Jooan — VR camera iterationWhen It Seen — 3-dancer body/face capture
05 / ACTION · FIGHT · IMPACT

Geography before speed; impact after clarity.

Combat, chase, physical action, impact beat, weapon / ability moment.

METHOD FRAMEWORK
Real-time + directing background

Action coverage has to answer “where is everyone?” before it tries to feel fast. I would establish screen geography and action axis, then use tighter impact angles only after the movement is readable. Camera shake, aggressive FOV changes, or rapid cuts should be earned by the impact beat rather than applied continuously.

FramingWide master for geography → medium tracking → impact close / insert.
MovementFollow action direction, preserve screen axis, accelerate only at motivated beats.
ImpactUse timing, proximity, framing change, effect readability, and reaction — not shake alone.
Edit NeedCapture clean ins / outs so marketing editors can shorten or extend the beat.
Coverage

Geography master → attacker medium → defender / target angle → tracking action → impact detail → reaction → aftermath / reset.

06 / GROUP · ENSEMBLE

Composition is the relationship between multiple subjects.

Squad, team, crowd, multi-character marketing image, synchronized entrances.

DIRECT PROOF
Row · Mythomania

A group shot fails when every character has equal visual weight. I look for hierarchy: who leads, who supports, where the eye enters, how silhouettes overlap, and whether timing creates readable layers instead of clutter.

FramingWide / medium-wide with deliberate spacing and silhouette separation.
BlockingUse depth, stagger, scale, and timing to create hierarchy.
LightingSeparate foreground / midground / background and prevent faces from merging.
VariantsFront, side, or alternate time-of-day coverage can change the read of the same group.
Coverage

Group master → alternate side / 3/4 → leader medium → subgroup / pair → individual detail → clean environment plate if useful.

Row — 10-character stagingMythomania — ensemble narrative coverage
07 / BEAUTY · CHARACTER HERO

Marketing clarity without losing character.

Hero portrait, character select, promotional beauty, armor / costume / face read.

DIRECT PROOF
Gang · Yaloo · Shininho

A hero shot is more controlled than a narrative shot. The priority is immediate character readability: face, silhouette, costume / material response, and visual identity. I would simplify camera movement and lighting complexity if either begins to compete with the subject.

FramingMedium / medium close / close-up depending what defines the character.
MovementStatic, subtle push, or slow orbit only when material / silhouette benefits.
LightingShape the face and material response; preserve silhouette and eye readability.
DeliveryCapture clean variants for stills, thumbnails, crop flexibility, and marketing layouts.
Coverage

Full / 3/4 hero → medium → close-up → material / prop detail → alternate light or background version.

08 / ENVIRONMENT · ATMOSPHERE

Let the world establish scale, mood, and direction.

Establishing, environment beauty, mood, traversal context, location reveal.

CURRENT PROOF
Jooan · Row lighting variants

Environment capture is not empty filler. It tells the audience where they are, how large the world feels, what direction matters, and what emotional temperature the scene begins with. Time of day and atmospheric contrast can do as much storytelling as camera movement.

FramingStrong depth layers, clear subject path, readable negative space.
MovementSlow reveal, restrained pan / truck, or static composition with environmental motion.
LightingTime-of-day is a narrative variable: dusk, morning, sunset, night change hierarchy.
DeliveryCapture clean plates / alternate conditions when campaign flexibility matters.
Coverage

Wide establishing → alternate direction → medium environmental subject → character-in-world version → atmosphere / detail insert.

Jooan — dusk HDRI / SkylightRow — morning / sunset / night
09 / EXIT · TRANSITION · AFTERMATH

End the shot with usable editorial information.

Exit, reset, aftermath, transition, hold, clean tail.

METHOD FRAMEWORK
Film / sequence thinking

Capture often becomes harder to edit because the camera or character stops too abruptly. I like to protect the end of a beat: let the subject leave frame, let the reaction settle, or hold the environment long enough to create a clean editorial tail.

FramingAllow space in the direction of exit or leave a stable composition after action ends.
MovementDecelerate or settle rather than cutting at the exact end of motion.
TimingHold a few usable beats after the primary action / reaction.
Edit NeedGive editors clean handles for cut points, transitions, titles, or sound design.
Coverage

Primary beat → reaction / follow-through → exit or settle → clean tail / environment hold.

SHOT LANGUAGE

The shot size is only useful if it solves a specific information problem.

I use shot size, movement, and coverage as a vocabulary. The point is not to collect “cool angles,” but to know why a wide, two-shot, OTS, close-up, insert, or tracking shot is the right tool for that moment.

WIDE / ESTABLISHING

Where are we?

Geography, scale, group relationship, traversal direction, or action axis.

MEDIUM / CHARACTER

What is the character doing?

Body language, gesture, weapon / prop interaction, readable performance without losing context.

CLOSE-UP / REACTION

What is the character feeling?

Eyes, expression, facial animation, emotional beat, decision, or reaction.

TWO-SHOT / OTS

What is the relationship?

Conversation, tension, listening, power balance, eyelines, and editorial dialogue coverage.

TRACKING / FOLLOW

Where is the action going?

Movement continuity, traversal, choreography, pursuit, or motivated character follow.

INSERT / DETAIL

What information cannot be missed?

Hands, object, weapon, ability, facial detail, interaction, or a cutaway that solves the edit.

CAPTURE PRINCIPLE

I do not force every scene into the same “beauty shot.” A performance scene needs body readability; a close-up needs facial detail and eye-line control; a group scene needs spatial separation and timing; a moving VR shot must first remain comfortable. The camera choice follows the scene problem.
