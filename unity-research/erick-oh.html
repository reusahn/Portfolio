Problem & individual contribution

Separate visual behavior from gameplay state.

The game needed expressive, changing forms that could respond to interaction and still remain controllable during art direction. A convincing image also had to remain understandable as a system: what changed in the object, what changed in the shader, and what affected interaction.

I implemented: C# runtime behavior, shader parameters, procedural deformation, and authored shape transitions.

I used Unity C# to connect interaction and runtime state to custom HLSL behavior. The documented work includes a procedural mesh cloud, a raymarched metaball exploration, and four authored growth states. These are distinct techniques with different control and collision requirements.

Implementation decisions

Why the system is structured this way.

01

Move the field, preserve the object

The archive describes input controlling the internal direction of a noise field. This gives visual motion without making the GameObject’s transform stand in for every kind of movement.

02

Keep rendering and contact separate

A raymarched field can visually merge shapes without providing a matching collider. The graphics exploration and interaction logic were kept separate. An attractive surface alone is insufficient evidence of a collision solution.

03

Use authored states where correspondence matters

The growth studies use fixed-topology Maya targets. Consistent vertex correspondence makes the intended shape changes explicit and editable rather than leaving every transition to procedural noise.

Debugging record

The documented implementation addresses a mismatch between visual movement and object movement by controlling the shader field separately. A dated before/after bug capture is still needed to establish the specific observed symptom, diagnosis, and outcome. No frame-time improvement is claimed.

A boundary you can inspect.

Implemented in this update / source only

The independent example integrates field phase in C# and passes it through a MaterialPropertyBlock. It uses simplified value noise rather than reproducing the production Perlin or metaball implementation. Rendering is URP-only. The rim uses the original mesh normals and is an illustrative unlit approximation.

Validation: These files were authored for this portfolio. They have not been compiled or executed in Unity. They are independent examples, not original client source.
